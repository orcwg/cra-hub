# If a steward also develops the software, doesn't that make it a manufacturer?

No. Developing the software does not make a steward its manufacturer; placing it on the market does. The steward definition says _"other than a manufacturer"_, and the Commission's guidance explains why: a manufacturer _"by definition, is the natural or legal person who places products with digital elements on the market ('markets them') under its own name or trademark"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]], [[CRA Article 3(14)]]). The manufacturer definition does say _"develops"_ and _"markets"_ ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]). But "markets them" means supplies them in the course of a commercial activity, so that the software is placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 47]]).

An organisation that develops and publishes open source software therefore shows it is not the manufacturer by showing it does not monetise that software. The guidance sets out the procedure: _"for each specific FOSS that it publishes, the legal person will need to ascertain whether that FOSS is considered to be placed on the market"_. If it is, the organisation is its manufacturer. If not, it may be its steward, provided the software is meant for commercial use and the organisation sustains it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]). What counts as monetising is in [[cra-itself/monetisation]].

The words _"free of charge"_ in the manufacturer definition do not change this. They cover software supplied without a price but monetised some other way, such as a free product through which paid services are sold, or one that requires personal data as a condition of use ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 54]]). A non-profit that even charges for its software stays a steward, as long as its earnings after costs serve its non-profit objectives ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 77]]).

For the two roles side by side, see [[stewards/manufacturer-vs-steward]]; for an organisation that is manufacturer of one project and steward of another, [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]].
