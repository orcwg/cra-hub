# Is an organisation that supports a project regularly but cannot set its security policy a steward?

No, not on that basis: regularly supporting a project does not make an organisation its steward unless it is also the one publishing the project and keeping it going, and an organisation that is a steward does not need the power to impose a security policy, because the CRA asks it to foster one rather than enforce it.

The Commission's guidance settles the first part. In its example, company B _"contributes code and developers' time to the FOSS's maintenance"_ of software that company A publishes and maintains: _"Company A is deemed to be the steward for that FOSS, whereas company B has no obligations under the CRA for that FOSS"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 31]]). The same goes for companies that contribute their developers' time to a project published by another company or by a foundation, or that fund features in it: the publisher is the steward and the contributing companies are not ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 28]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]). The reason is that software is under the responsibility of those who publish it and control its development, releases and distribution, not of those who contribute to it, even with commit access, and the steward is the organisation that publishes the software without selling it and sustains it ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). Funding a project does not make the funder its manufacturer either: see [[manufacturers/funding-foss-dev]] and [[manufacturers/contributors]].

Regular contributions or financing from a company do matter, but for the project, not for the company. The guidance says foundations whose platforms _"enable manufacturers to contribute regularly to the development of FOSS and/or that are regularly financed by manufacturers"_ are the stewards of the projects they sustain ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]): regular contributions or funding from manufacturers are a sign that the software is intended for commercial activities, which is one of the conditions for the organisation publishing it to be its steward ([[CRA Recital 19]]).

On the second part, neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance says whether a steward has to be able to set the project's security policy. The hub's reading is that it does not. If your organisation is the one publishing and sustaining the project, you are its steward even if the project's governance keeps you from dictating its policies. The policy the CRA requires is one _"to foster the development of a secure product with digital elements as well as an effective handling of vulnerabilities by the developers of that product"_, and it must _"take into account the specific nature of the open-source software steward and the legal and organisational arrangements to which it is subject"_ ([[CRA Article 24(1)]]). The guidance counts organisations that provide only non-technical support, such as managing the project's branding, laying down governance rules or collecting donations, as stewards ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]), and expects such a steward that learns of an actively exploited vulnerability to pass it on to the maintainers in line with its policy ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 80]]). The policy describes what you do to encourage secure development and vulnerability handling within your arrangements with the project; it is not a rule you have to be able to impose on the maintainers. For the steward's obligations, see [[stewards/obligations]]; for what the policy covers, see [[stewards/policy-scope]].

For how stewardship attaches to a project, see [[stewards/relationship]].
