# Is an organisation that supports a project regularly but cannot set its security policy a steward?

No, not on that basis: regularly supporting a project does not make an organisation its steward unless it is also the one publishing the project and keeping it going, and an organisation that is a steward does not need the power to impose a security policy, because the CRA asks it to foster one rather than enforce it.

Stewardship attaches to the organisation that publishes the project without selling it and sustains it ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). The CRA does not apply to those who contribute code to software that is not under their responsibility ([[CRA Recital 18]]), and software is under the responsibility of those who publish it and control its development, releases and distribution, not of those who contribute to it, even with commit access ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). The Commission's guidance gives the examples: companies that contribute their developers' time to a project published by a foundation or by another company have no obligations under the CRA for that project, and the foundation or company publishing it is the steward ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 31]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]). Funding a project does not make the funder its manufacturer either: see [[manufacturers/funding-foss-dev]] and [[manufacturers/contributors]].

Regular contributions or financing from a company do matter, but for the project, not for the company: they are one of the signs that the software is intended for commercial activities, which is one of the conditions for the organisation publishing it to be its steward ([[CRA Recital 19]]).

If your organisation is the one publishing and sustaining the project, you are its steward even if the project's governance keeps you from dictating its policies. The policy the CRA requires is one _"to foster the development of a secure product with digital elements as well as an effective handling of vulnerabilities by the developers of that product"_, and it must _"take into account the specific nature of the open-source software steward and the legal and organisational arrangements to which it is subject"_ ([[CRA Article 24(1)]]). The guidance counts organisations that provide only non-technical support, such as managing the project's branding, laying down governance rules or collecting donations, as stewards ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]), and expects such a steward that learns of an actively exploited vulnerability to pass it on to the maintainers in line with its policy ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 80]]). The policy describes what you do to encourage secure development and vulnerability handling within your arrangements with the project; it is not a rule you have to be able to impose on the maintainers. For the steward's obligations, see [[stewards/obligations]]; for what the policy covers, see [[stewards/policy-scope]].

For how stewardship attaches to a project, see [[stewards/relationship]].
