# Does a steward bear the cost of translating and maintaining its policy documents in many of the EU languages?

No, a steward does not have to translate its cybersecurity policy into many EU languages or keep translations up to date. You can write and maintain the policy in one language of your choice. Translation only comes into play if a market surveillance authority asks for the policy and cannot easily read it in that language, and then only for that authority and at that time.

Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance on stewards ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Section 3.3]]) says anything about the language of a steward's policy. The Commission's FAQ does answer the same question for a manufacturer's technical documentation: it _"can be written in any language. Nevertheless, if it is required by a market surveillance authority, it needs to be provided in a language easily understood by this authority"_ ([[official/faq_6-6]]). The CRA's rule for a steward's policy is worded the same way: the policy has to be documented _"in a verifiable manner"_ ([[CRA Article 24(1)]]), and an authority gets it _"further to a reasoned request"_, _"in a language which can be easily understood by that authority"_ ([[CRA Article 24(2)]]). This is a duty to respond when asked, not a duty to prepare translations in advance.

Which language an authority can _"easily understand"_ is for that authority to say: the CRA does not require the official language of its country, and it does not say which country's authority will ask. If a request comes, the steward provides the policy in that language, at its own cost. For most stewards this is a one-off translation for one authority, if it ever arises at all.

The policy is one of a steward's few obligations under the CRA: see [[stewards/obligations]].
