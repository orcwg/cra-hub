# Is stewardship something you opt in to, or can you be designated as a steward, and if so, by whom?

Neither. Your organisation is an _open-source software steward_ when it meets the definition, by what it does, not by declaring itself one or being named one: a company or foundation (a _legal person_), other than a manufacturer, that has the purpose or objective of systematically providing sustained support for specific open source software intended for commercial use, and that ensures its viability ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]). Where that is the case, _"that legal person is subject to the obligations of stewards"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]]).

Nobody designates stewards. The CRA gives no authority, project or other organisation the power to appoint one, and regulators will not nominate a steward for a project that has none: see [[stewards/nominations]]. A project calling a foundation its steward doesn't make it one, and a foundation hosting a project isn't its steward unless it gives that project systematic support and ensures its viability ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]).

There is no opting out either. For each project you publish, you have to work out whether you place it on the market, which makes you its manufacturer, or sustain it without doing so, which makes you its steward ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]). What counts as sustained support: see [[stewards/sustained-support]]. The role only reaches organisations; an individual can't be a steward, whatever support they give: see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]].

The choices you do have are about the facts: whether to take on the sustained support of a project, and whether to monetise it. For what being a steward requires, see [[stewards/obligations]].
