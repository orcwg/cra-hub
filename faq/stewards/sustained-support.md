# What does 'systematically providing support on a sustained basis' mean for an open-source software steward?

'Systematically providing support on a sustained basis' means your organisation keeps specific open source projects going as an ongoing commitment, rather than contributing to them now and then. The Commission's guidance says the support stewards give _"can vary greatly"_ and sorts it into three kinds, each of which counts ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]):

- **Non-technical support**: managing the project's branding, laying down governance rules, organising community events, collecting donations.
- **Infrastructure**: hosting source code repositories, providing version control, generating signing keys.
- **Engineering**: employing developers, coordinating development, reviewing or merging code, managing releases, handling vulnerability reports and security patches.

The CRA's own list, which the guidance repeats, is open-ended: hosting and managing collaboration platforms, hosting source code or software, governing or managing the projects, and steering their development ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 75]]). Alongside the support, a steward _"ensures the viability"_ of the projects it supports ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]).

Every steward has to put a cybersecurity policy in place and cooperate with market surveillance authorities. Whether it also has to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents depends on which of these kinds of support it provides, and only from 11 December 2027 ([[CRA Article 24]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]], [[official/faq_5-5]]): see [[stewards/notification-obligations]].

Sustained support is judged project by project. The Commission's guidance says a foundation is the steward of the specific projects it systematically supports and keeps viable, not of everything it happens to host, and that a company can be the steward of one project and the manufacturer of another ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 72]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]). Contributing code or funding to a project you do not control does not make you its steward: the guidance's example is a company that contributes code and developers' time to another company's project and has no obligations for it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 31]]).

Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance says whether declaring that you promise no support changes anything. In the hub's reading it does not: what counts is what your organisation actually does for the project. The guidance's conditions are factual, whether you provide systematic support and ensure the project's viability, and when you stop providing sustained support you may stop being its steward; the Commission encourages you to say so clearly when that happens ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 83]]). See [[stewards/inactive]] for what happens when a stewarded project goes inactive.

Sustained support is one of three conditions. The software also has to be intended for commercial activities, see [[stewards/business-context]], and the steward has to be a company or foundation rather than an individual, see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]].
