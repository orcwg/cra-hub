# Can a company avoid manufacturer obligations by declaring itself an open-source software steward?

No, a company cannot avoid its manufacturer obligations by declaring itself an _open-source software steward_. The Commission's guidance says which role you have follows from what you do with each piece of software, not from what you call yourself: _"For each specific FOSS that it publishes, the legal person will need to ascertain whether that FOSS is considered to be placed on the market within the meaning of the CRA. If so, that makes the legal entity the software's manufacturer. If the FOSS is not considered to be placed on the market, the legal person may be the steward to it"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]).

In plain terms: you are the manufacturer of any software you sell or otherwise monetise under your name or brand, and the manufacturer's obligations of [[CRA Article 13]] follow ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]]). The steward role exists only for open source software that is published but not sold, and the guidance says the two roles cannot overlap on the same software: _"A steward is defined as 'a legal person, other than a manufacturer' because a manufacturer, by definition, is the natural or legal person who places products with digital elements on the market"_ ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). Charging a price is not the only way to monetise software; the others are listed in [[cra-itself/monetisation]].

Stewardship is therefore not a lighter alternative you can choose instead of being a manufacturer. It is a separate set of obligations for companies and foundations that publish open source software without selling it, software that would be outside the CRA altogether if an individual published it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 43]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]). Developing the software does not by itself make you its manufacturer; placing it on the market does ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 47]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). So a company that writes code for a project it publishes without monetising it can be that project's steward, and shows it by showing that the project is not monetised: not sold, no paid-only version or updates, no services or personal data monetised through it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Section 3.2]]).

The same company can hold both roles for different software, or for different versions of the same project: manufacturer of the paid edition, steward of the free "community" edition ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 73]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 76]]). See [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]]. A steward that starts to monetise a project becomes its manufacturer from the day it does ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 84]]).
