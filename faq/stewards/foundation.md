# Can a manufacturer set up a foundation that acts as an open-source software steward for community-led projects?

Yes, a manufacturer can set up a foundation that acts as an _open-source software steward_ for community-led projects, but it does not need one: a manufacturer can be a steward itself.

The Commission's guidance has foundations financed and staffed by manufacturers in mind when it describes stewards: _"certain foundations offer collaboration platforms with various forms of governance that enable manufacturers to contribute regularly to the development of FOSS and/or that are regularly financed by manufacturers. Such foundations are to be considered stewards in relation to specific FOSS which are intended for commercial activities and for which the foundation offers sustained support"_ ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]). Its examples include a foundation funded by membership fees whose code is written by developers the members employ, and one to which companies lend their developers' time; in both, the foundation is the steward and the companies owe only due diligence when they integrate the project ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 28]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 33]]). Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance addresses the case where one manufacturer sets the foundation up or controls it. The hub reads them as covering it all the same: how a project is financed does not count towards whether it is placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 64]]), and who wrote the code does not decide who its steward is: see [[stewards/whose-codebase]].

A separate foundation is not required, because the guidance says a manufacturer can be a steward too. The roles are decided per piece of software: a company is the manufacturer of the software it sells, and can at the same time be the steward of open source software it publishes without selling, including the free "community" version of the very product it sells ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 73]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 76]]). See [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]].

What a foundation cannot do is take the manufacturer's obligations off the manufacturer. The guidance says manufacturers that integrate open source components _"are required to comply with the CRA for their own products with digital elements"_ and owe due diligence on those components ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 33]]); the Commission's FAQ says the same of components published by a steward: the manufacturer may integrate them, and exercises due diligence on them ([[official/faq_4-4-4]]). Whoever sells or otherwise monetises a product is its manufacturer, whatever other organisation supports the project it is built from ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]): see [[stewards/avoid-manufacturer-obligations]]. The foundation is the steward only of what it actually publishes and supports on a sustained basis ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]).
