# Does being a CNA (CVE Numbering Authority) for an open source project make you its steward?

No, being a CNA for an open source project does not, by itself, make you its _open-source software steward_. Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance mentions CNAs. What the Commission's guidance does settle is what makes a company or foundation a steward: it is the legal person that publishes an open source project without selling it and _"has the purpose or objective of systematically providing support on a sustained basis for the development of [FOSS] […] intended for commercial activities, and that ensures the viability of those products"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]], [[CRA Article 3(14)]]), and _"for each specific FOSS that it publishes, the legal person will need to ascertain"_ for itself whether it meets that definition ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]). Nothing in it turns on a title or a role such as CNA; what counts is what you actually do for the project. The definition in full: [[stewards/what-is-a-steward]]; who fits: [[stewards/who-can-be-steward]].

On that reading, assigning CVE identifiers is a narrow service. A CNA does it for vulnerabilities in a project, whether the CNA is a third party, a company involved in the project or the project itself, and nothing in that role means the CNA publishes the project, supports its development or keeps it going. The guidance does list _"handling vulnerability reports and security patches"_ among the ways a steward that contributes engineering resources to a project supports it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]), so a company that is the CNA for a project it also publishes and sustains will usually be its steward; but it is the publishing and the sustained support that make it one, not the CNA role.

Most open source projects have no steward at all, CNA or not: see [[stewards/do-all-projects-have-stewards]].
