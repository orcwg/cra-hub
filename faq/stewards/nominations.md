# Will regulators nominate a steward for an important or critical open source project that has none?

No, regulators will not nominate or appoint a steward for an open source project that has none, however important the project is. The CRA gives no authority that power: a company or foundation is an _open-source software steward_ because it meets the definition in [[CRA Article 3(14)]], not because someone chose it, and the Commission's guidance says each company or foundation works out for itself, project by project, whether it meets that definition ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]). One that decides to take on sustained support for a project becomes its steward by doing so, with the obligations that follow ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]]); that is its own choice, not one made for it.

What authorities can do is check on those who are stewards. Market surveillance authorities oversee the steward obligations in [[CRA Article 24]] and can require a steward that does not meet them to take corrective action ([[CRA Article 52(3)]]); stewards have to cooperate with them and hand over their cybersecurity policy on request ([[CRA Article 24(2)]]). Nothing in the CRA lets an authority turn a company or foundation that does not meet the definition into a steward, or require one to take on a project. What to expect from authorities: [[stewards/enforcement]].

A project with no steward simply has none under the CRA, and most open source projects are in that position: see [[stewards/do-all-projects-have-stewards]]. Its maintainers have no obligations as long as they do not sell it ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). The companies that integrate it into products they sell are the ones the CRA holds responsible: they have to exercise due diligence on the component ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]]), and the Commission can set up voluntary security attestation programmes to help them, which those companies can themselves initiate or finance ([[CRA Article 25]], [[CRA Recital 21]]). What that due diligence involves: [[due-diligence/foss]] and [[official/faq_4-4-4]].

The important and critical product categories change none of this. They decide which conformity assessment a manufacturer has to follow before selling a product ([[CRA Article 7(1)]], [[CRA Article 8(1)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 137]]). They do not apply to a project that is not sold, which is outside the CRA ([[CRA Recital 18]]), and they add nothing to a steward's obligations in [[CRA Article 24]].
