# Will regulators nominate a steward for an important or critical open source project that has none?

No, regulators will not nominate or appoint a steward for an open source project that has none, however important the project is. Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance provides for anyone to appoint a steward. What the Commission's guidance says is that a company or foundation is an _open-source software steward_ because of what it does, and that it works this out for itself: _"For each specific FOSS that it publishes, the legal person will need to ascertain whether that FOSS is considered to be placed on the market within the meaning of the CRA. […] If the FOSS is not considered to be placed on the market, the legal person may be the steward to it, if the software is intended for commercial activities and if the legal person is sustaining it in line with the definition of steward"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]], [[CRA Article 3(14)]]). One that decides to take on sustained support for a project becomes its steward by doing so, with the obligations that follow ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]]); that is its own choice, not one made for it, and the CRA gives no authority the power to make it.

What authorities can do is check on those who are stewards. Market surveillance authorities oversee the steward obligations in [[CRA Article 24]] and can require a steward that does not meet them to take corrective action ([[CRA Article 52(3)]]); stewards have to cooperate with them and hand over their cybersecurity policy on request ([[CRA Article 24(2)]]). Nothing in the CRA lets an authority turn a company or foundation that does not meet the definition into a steward, or require one to take on a project. What to expect from authorities: [[stewards/enforcement]].

A project with no steward simply has none under the CRA, and most open source projects are in that position: see [[stewards/do-all-projects-have-stewards]]. The Commission's guidance says its maintainers have no obligations as long as they do not sell it: _"Maintainers of FOSS components that are not placed on the market do not bear obligations in relation to other entities that may integrate such components into their products with digital elements"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). The companies that integrate it into products they sell are the ones the CRA holds responsible. The Commission's FAQ says they _"are allowed to integrate open-source components that are not in scope of the CRA"_, with due diligence proportionate to the component's risk, and that the Commission can set up voluntary security attestation programmes to help them ([[official/faq_4-4-4]], [[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Article 25]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]]). What that due diligence involves: [[due-diligence/foss]].

The important and critical product categories change none of this. The Commission's FAQ says they are about the products manufacturers sell: _"a manufacturer should look at the core functionality of its product with digital elements to determine whether that product is an important or critical product with digital elements and is therefore subject to the corresponding conformity assessment procedures"_ ([[official/faq_3-1]], [[CRA Article 7(1)]], [[CRA Article 8(1)]]). They do not apply to a project that is not sold, which is outside the CRA ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]), and they add nothing to a steward's obligations in [[CRA Article 24]].
