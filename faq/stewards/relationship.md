# What is the relationship between a project and its steward?

A steward is the company or foundation that publishes a project without selling it and keeps it going: it is the steward of that specific project, not a steward in general, and whether it is one is worked out project by project.

The Commission's guidance says so directly. The concept of steward _"applies to specific instances of FOSS"_ that are intended for commercial activities but not sold, _"and for which the legal person publishing that FOSS ensures systematic support"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). The relationship is per project: _"Being a steward for one specific FOSS does not mean that that legal person is necessarily also a steward for other FOSS that it publishes"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 72]]). For each piece of software it publishes, an organisation has to work out whether it is selling it, in which case it is the manufacturer; stewarding it, because it publishes it without selling it, the software is intended for commercial use and the organisation sustains it; or neither ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]). A foundation that hosts many projects is the steward of those it sustains and that are intended for commercial activities, and may have no CRA obligations at all for the others ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]). The same company can be the manufacturer of the paid edition of a project and the steward of its community edition: see [[stewards/for-profit]] and [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]].

"Support" covers more than code. The guidance lists hosting the code or the collaboration platform, governing or managing the project and steering its development ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 75]]), and counts as stewards organisations whose support is purely non-technical, such as managing the project's branding, laying down its governance rules or collecting donations, as well as those that employ its developers, manage its releases and handle its security patches ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]). The definition itself, a legal person (a company, foundation or association, not an individual) that systematically sustains specific open source software intended for commercial activities and ensures its viability, is in [[CRA Article 3(14)]]: see [[stewards/what-is-a-steward]].

Neither the CRA nor the Commission's guidance defines a "project", or says what ties a steward to one beyond publishing it and sustaining it. The hub's reading is that the CRA looks at specific software, and at who publishes it and controls its development, releases and distribution ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). Who holds the copyright does not decide stewardship: you can be the steward of your own code or of someone else's (see [[stewards/whose-codebase]]). Nor does a trademark: the CRA mentions trademarks only to identify the manufacturer of software sold under a name or brand ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). Governance comes closest to how the CRA sees it: the steward is the organisation under which the project is published and sustained, and governing, managing or steering a project is one of the forms that support takes ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 75]]).

Because the relationship is a matter of fact, a project does not pick its steward and an organisation does not sign up for the role: see [[stewards/choosing]]. A company that only contributes code or money to a project someone else publishes is not its steward: see [[stewards/support-without-control]]. For whether a project can have several stewards, see [[stewards/multiple]]; for what a steward has to do, see [[stewards/obligations]].
