# What is the relationship between a project and its steward?

A steward is the company or foundation that publishes a project without selling it and keeps it going: it is the steward of that specific project, not a steward in general, and whether it is one is worked out project by project.

The CRA defines an _open-source software steward_ as a legal person (a company, foundation or association, not an individual) _"that has the purpose or objective of systematically providing support on a sustained basis for the development of specific products with digital elements"_ that are open source and intended for commercial activities, _"and that ensures the viability of those products"_ ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]). The Commission's guidance reads this as the legal person that publishes the software without placing it on the market and _"ensures systematic support"_ for it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). Support can mean hosting the code or the collaboration platform, governing or managing the project, or steering its development ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 75]]). It ranges from purely non-technical support, such as managing the project's branding, laying down its governance rules or collecting donations, to employing its developers, managing its releases and handling its security patches ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]).

The relationship is per project. _"Being a steward for one specific FOSS does not mean that that legal person is necessarily also a steward for other FOSS that it publishes"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 72]]). For each piece of software it publishes, an organisation has to work out whether it is selling it, in which case it is the manufacturer; stewarding it, because it publishes it without selling it, the software is intended for commercial use and the organisation sustains it; or neither ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 73]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]). A foundation that hosts many projects is the steward of those it sustains and that are intended for commercial activities, and may have no CRA obligations at all for the others ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]). The same company can be the manufacturer of the paid edition of a project and the steward of its community edition: see [[stewards/for-profit]] and [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]].

The CRA does not define a "project". It looks at specific software, and at who publishes it and controls its development, releases and distribution ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). Who holds the copyright does not decide stewardship: you can be the steward of your own code or of someone else's (see [[stewards/whose-codebase]]). Nor does a trademark: the CRA mentions trademarks only to identify the manufacturer of software sold under a name or brand ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). Governance comes closest to how the CRA sees it: the steward is the organisation under which the project is published and sustained, and governing, managing or steering a project is one of the forms that support takes ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 75]]).

Because the relationship is a matter of fact, a project does not pick its steward and an organisation does not sign up for the role: see [[stewards/choosing]]. A company that only contributes code or money to a project someone else publishes is not its steward: see [[stewards/support-without-control]]. For whether a project can have several stewards, see [[stewards/multiple]]; for what a steward has to do, see [[stewards/obligations]].
