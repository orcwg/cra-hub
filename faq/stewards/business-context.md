# What does 'intended for commercial activities' (or 'in a business context') mean in the steward definition?

Open source software is _"intended for commercial activities"_ when it is meant to end up in something that is sold: the CRA's examples are _"integration into commercial services or into monetised products with digital elements"_, whether by the organisation that publishes the software or by other companies ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]). "In a business context" is the same idea seen from the publisher's side: stewards include _"entities that develop and publish free and open-source software in a business context"_ ([[CRA Recital 19]]).

This condition only matters for companies and foundations. An individual cannot be a steward whatever their software is used for, and the open source software they publish without monetising it is outside the CRA altogether ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]): see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]].

For a company or foundation, the Commission's guidance gives these signs that software is intended for commercial activities:

- You built it for your own products or services, which you sell, and publish it as open source alongside them ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 76]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 25]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]]).
- You publish it for other manufacturers to build into their products: a library, an SDK, a reference implementation ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 67]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 26]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 33]]).
- Manufacturers who integrate it contribute to its development regularly, or fund it regularly to keep it going ([[CRA Recital 19]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]).

What does not, by itself, make software intended for commercial activities:

- **Using it yourself at work.** Using a tool in your own job, whether you are a freelancer or a company, is not building it into a service or product that customers get. Software made for one's own use is not placed on the market ([[official/faq_1-5]]), and publishing it as open source without monetising it does not change that ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 42]]).
- **Publishing it under an open source licence.** Every open source licence allows commercial use. The guidance treats some of the software a foundation hosts as _"not intended for commercial activities"_, so the licence alone cannot be what decides ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]).
- **Someone else integrating it.** The mere fact that a manufacturer integrates your software into its product changes nothing about your status ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). What the CRA counts is regular contributions or regular funding from the manufacturers who integrate it ([[CRA Recital 19]]).

Meeting this condition does not make your organisation a manufacturer: you are a manufacturer only for software you actually sell or otherwise monetise ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]). It makes you a steward, with the lighter obligations of [[CRA Article 24]], provided your organisation also supports the project on a sustained basis: see [[stewards/sustained-support]] and [[stewards/obligations]].
