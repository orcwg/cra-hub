# How do _open-source software stewards_ demonstrate that they meet their obligations?

By showing their cybersecurity policy, and that the project follows it. The CRA asks a steward to _"put in place and document in a verifiable manner"_ that policy ([[CRA Article 24(1)]]). On a reasoned request from a market surveillance authority, the steward provides that documentation, on paper or electronically, in a language the authority can easily understand ([[CRA Article 24(2)]]). That is the whole of it. A steward has no conformity assessment to pass, no CE marking and no technical documentation to prepare: see [[stewards/documentation]] and [[stewards/essential-requirements]].

Neither the CRA nor the Commission's guidance says what makes the documentation verifiable. The word points to two things an authority can check. The policy exists, with a date and a history, in a place the authority can be sent to, such as a public repository. And the project does what the policy says, which its public record shows: a published disclosure process and contact, security advisories, fixed vulnerabilities recorded as such. ORC WG's white paper suggests keeping the policy under version control and linking each of its statements to that kind of evidence: see [[stewards/white-paper]].

For what the policy has to contain, see [[stewards/policy-content]]; for evidence specific to vulnerability reporting, [[stewards/evidence]]; for what to expect from an authority, [[stewards/enforcement]].
