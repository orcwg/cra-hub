# Is my company the steward of the open source software it publishes?

Yes, in most cases. Open source software that your company publishes without being its manufacturer, and uses to support its products, its services or other parts of its business, is meant for commercial use, and your company is its steward. The definition asks for a company or foundation, other than a manufacturer, that systematically provides sustained support for specific open source software intended for commercial activities and ensures its viability ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]). The Commission's guidance says the role covers software _"ultimately intended for commercial activities, such as for integration into commercial services or into monetised products"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]), and gives this very case: a company that develops open source software for its own products and publishes it without placing it on the market _"is not a manufacturer of the software, but may be a steward to it"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 76]]). Publishing and maintaining it is the sustained support the definition asks for ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 75]]; see [[stewards/sustained-support]]).

The role is decided project by project ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]; see [[stewards/relationship]]). If the company sells a version of the software, it is that version's manufacturer and the steward of the free one ([[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]]). The software may be the company's own ([[stewards/whose-codebase]]), and developing it does not make the company its manufacturer ([[stewards/developing]]). For the commercial-use test, see [[stewards/business-context]].

The role brings the obligations of [[CRA Article 24]] from 11 December 2027 ([[stewards/obligations]], [[stewards/start-date]]). A steward cannot be fined for failing them ([[CRA Article 64(10)(b)]]; see [[stewards/penalties]]), but the company can be fined as the manufacturer of the products that contain the software: a manufacturer has to comply for its product as a whole, components included, and a vulnerability left unhandled in the software reaches it there ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]], [[official/faq_4-3-6]]; see [[manufacturers/mistakes]]). That is the reason to take the steward role seriously: the security policy and vulnerability handling it asks for are what keep the company's own products compliant.
