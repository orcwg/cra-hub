# Is my company the steward of the open source software it publishes?

Yes, your company is the steward of the open source software it publishes if it does not sell it, means it for commercial use and supports it on a sustained basis. Those are the parts of the definition: an _open-source software steward_ is a company or foundation which is systematically providing sustained support for the development of specific open source software intended for commercial activities, and which ensures that software's viability ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]). The Commission's guidance says a company that develops open source software for its own products and publishes it without placing it on the market _"is not a manufacturer of the software, but may be a steward to it"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 76]]), and that a company which fits the definition _"is subject to the obligations of stewards"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]]).

Not selling it means not monetising it ([[maintainers/become-manufacturer]]); a company that sells one version and publishes another for free is the manufacturer of the first and the steward of the second ([[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]]). Software is intended for commercial use when it is meant to go into paid products, your own included ([[stewards/business-context]]). Sustained support is the publishing, hosting, governing or steering of the project ([[stewards/sustained-support]]). The software may be your own ([[stewards/whose-codebase]]), and developing it does not make you its manufacturer ([[stewards/developing]]).

The role is decided project by project ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]; see [[stewards/relationship]]). Where it applies, it brings the obligations of [[CRA Article 24]] from 11 December 2027 ([[stewards/obligations]], [[stewards/start-date]]). A steward cannot be fined ([[stewards/penalties]]), but the products your company sells with that software in them have to comply as a whole ([[manufacturers/mistakes]]).
