# Can a company be classified as an open-source software steward?

Yes, a company can be an open-source software steward. The CRA defines a steward as _"a legal person, other than a manufacturer"_ that systematically provides sustained support for the development of free and open-source software intended for commercial activities ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 70]]). [[CRA Recital 19]] adds: _"Open-source software stewards include certain foundations as well as entities that develop and publish free and open-source software in a business context, **including not-for-profit entities.**"_ "Other than a manufacturer" means a company is a steward only for software it publishes without selling it; the same company can be the manufacturer of other software it sells ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 73]]).

A company can even be a steward for a project that also has a commercial version. The company is then the **manufacturer** of the paid or monetised version, with the manufacturer's obligations, and at the same time the **steward** of the free or "community" version that it publishes without monetising ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 52]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 76]]).

This dual role is possible because the CRA looks at each piece of software separately ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 72]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]):

- **Manufacturer obligations** apply to the paid or monetised version.
- **Steward obligations** apply to the free open source version and focus on fostering secure development and effective vulnerability handling for its users.

For more details on how manufacturers and stewards interact, see [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]].
