# Will regulators nominate a steward for an important or critical open source project that has none?

No, regulators will not find or nominate a steward for an open source project that has none, however important the project. Neither the CRA nor the Commission's FAQ or guidance provides any way for an authority to identify, nominate or appoint an _open-source software steward_.

Being a steward follows from what a company or foundation does, not from a decision by an authority. The Commission's guidance says that _"for each specific FOSS that it publishes, the legal person will need to ascertain"_ for itself whether it meets the definition ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]); authorities can only check that stewards meet their obligations: see [[stewards/enforcement]].

Such a project simply has no steward under the CRA, even one classified as important or critical. The companies that integrate it into products they sell still have to exercise due diligence on it, as the Commission's FAQ says they _"are allowed to integrate open-source components that are not in scope of the CRA"_ ([[official/faq_4-4-4]], [[CRA Article 13(5)]]): see [[due-diligence/foss]].

What makes an entity a steward: [[stewards/what-is-a-steward]]. Most projects have none: [[stewards/do-all-projects-have-stewards]].
