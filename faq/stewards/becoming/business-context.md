# What does 'in a business context' mean for the steward definition?

Open source software is published "in a business context" when it is meant to be used commercially, such as built into paid products or commercial services. The phrase comes from [[CRA Recital 19]], which describes stewards; the definition itself, in [[CRA Article 3(14)]], says the software must be _"intended for commercial activities"_.

The Commission's guidance treats the two as one idea: the steward role applies to open source software that is _"ultimately intended for commercial activities, such as for integration into commercial services or into monetised products with digital elements"_ but is not placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]], [[CRA Recital 19]]).

So the test is who the software is for. A company that builds a component into what it sells and contributes to it regularly, or funds it regularly, shows that intention ([[CRA Recital 19]]). A project nobody is expected to use commercially is not in a business context, and an organisation supporting it has no steward obligations for it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]]).

The test only ever applies to a company or foundation (a _legal person_), never to an individual maintainer: see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]]. For the rest of the definition, see [[stewards/what-is-a-steward]].
