# Does a steward bear the cost of translating and maintaining its policy documents in many of the EU languages?

No, you do not have to translate your cybersecurity policy into many EU languages or keep translations up to date. You can write and maintain the policy in one language of your choice.

The CRA requires stewards to put in place and document a cybersecurity policy, but says nothing about its language ([[CRA Article 24(1)]]). The Commission's guidance and FAQ are silent on this for stewards; the FAQ does answer it for a manufacturer's technical documentation, which _"can be written in any language. Nevertheless, if it is required by a market surveillance authority, it needs to be provided in a language easily understood by this authority"_ ([[official/faq_6-6]]).

The rule for a steward's policy is the same: translation comes into play only when a market surveillance authority makes a reasoned request, and you then provide the policy _"in a language which can be easily understood by that authority"_ ([[CRA Article 24(2)]]). This is a duty to respond when asked, not a general obligation to keep multilingual versions.

The CRA does not say who pays for that translation; as you are the one who has to provide the document, the cost is yours, a one-off for one authority, if it ever arises.

The policy is one of a steward's few obligations under the CRA: see [[stewards/obligations]].
