# What happens when an _open-source software steward_ doesn't meet its obligations?

A steward that doesn't meet its obligations can be required by a market surveillance authority to put things right, but it cannot be fined: the CRA's administrative fines do not apply to _"any infringement of this Regulation by open-source software stewards"_ ([[CRA Article 64(10)(b)]]).

What an authority can do is order corrective action. Market surveillance authorities are in charge of checking that stewards meet their obligations under [[CRA Article 24]], and where one finds that a steward doesn't, it _"shall require the open-source software steward to ensure that all appropriate corrective actions are taken"_; the steward must then take them ([[CRA Article 52(3)]]). In practice that means fixing what was missing: writing down or publishing the cybersecurity policy, answering a request for it, or reporting a vulnerability or incident that should have been reported. What the obligations are: [[stewards/obligations]]. What enforcement is likely to look like in practice: [[stewards/enforcement]].

Fines of any kind are off the table, not just the administrative fines of [[CRA Article 64]]: the CRA tells Member States not to _"impose other kinds of penalties with pecuniary character"_ on stewards ([[CRA Recital 120]]). Member States still have to lay down penalties for infringements of the CRA that are _"effective, proportionate and dissuasive"_ ([[CRA Article 64(1)]]), and market surveillance authorities act under their own national rules, so ignoring an authority's order can have non-financial consequences under national law. The CRA itself specifies none for stewards beyond the corrective action.

Nobody "strips" a steward of its status; the CRA has no procedure for it. An organisation is the steward of a project as long as it systematically supports that project on a sustained basis and ensures its viability ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]), and stops being one when it stops supporting it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 83]]): see [[stewards/inactive]]. Failing to meet steward obligations doesn't turn a steward into a manufacturer either; that only happens if it starts monetising the software ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 84]]).
