# What happens when an _open-source software steward_ doesn't meet its obligations?

A steward that doesn't meet its obligations can be required by a market surveillance authority to put things right, but it cannot be fined. The Commission's Q&A gives the general rule: market surveillance authorities enforce the CRA, _"could require operators to bring the non-compliance to an end"_ and _"will be able to fine companies that do not adhere to the rules"_ ([[cra-basics/what-will-happen-to-non-compliant-products]]). For stewards, the CRA itself takes the fines out: its administrative fines do not apply to _"any infringement of this Regulation by open-source software stewards"_ ([[CRA Article 64(10)(b)]]). Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance says anything more specific about a steward that falls short; the rest rests on the CRA's text.

What an authority can do is order corrective action. Where a market surveillance authority finds that a steward doesn't meet its obligations under [[CRA Article 24]], it _"shall require the open-source software steward to ensure that all appropriate corrective actions are taken"_ ([[CRA Article 52(3)]]). In practice that means fixing what was missing: writing down or publishing the cybersecurity policy, answering a request for it, or reporting a vulnerability or incident that should have been reported. What the obligations are: [[stewards/obligations]]. What enforcement is likely to look like in practice: [[stewards/enforcement]].

Fines of any kind are off the table, not just the administrative fines of [[CRA Article 64]]: the CRA tells Member States not to _"impose other kinds of penalties with pecuniary character"_ on stewards ([[CRA Recital 120]]). Member States still have to lay down penalties for infringements of the CRA that are _"effective, proportionate and dissuasive"_ ([[CRA Article 64(1)]]), and market surveillance authorities act under their own national rules, so ignoring an authority's order can have non-financial consequences under national law. The CRA itself specifies none for stewards beyond the corrective action.

Nobody "strips" a steward of its status; the CRA has no procedure for it. An organisation is the steward of a project as long as it systematically supports that project on a sustained basis and ensures its viability ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]), and the Commission's guidance says that one that _"ceases to provide systematic support on a sustained basis"_ for a project _"may no longer meet the definition of steward"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 83]]): see [[stewards/inactive]]. Failing to meet steward obligations doesn't turn a steward into a manufacturer either; the guidance says that only happens if it starts monetising the software ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 84]]).
