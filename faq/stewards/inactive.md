# What happens if a project under a steward becomes inactive?

If a project you steward goes inactive because all its contributors have left, you can stop stewarding it, and once you no longer provide sustained support for it you are no longer its steward under the CRA. The Commission's guidance says so: where an organisation _"ceases to provide systematic support on a sustained basis for a specific FOSS, it may no longer meet the definition of steward and may therefore no longer be subject to the corresponding obligations"_, and it _"is encouraged to communicate clearly the change in its status for that specific FOSS"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 83]]).

What the guidance turns on is whether you still support the project, not whether anyone else is still active. You are a project's steward while you have _"the purpose or objective of systematically providing support on a sustained basis"_ for it and ensure its viability ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]). While you keep supporting it (hosting it, managing its releases, employing its developers), you remain its steward and your obligations continue, contributors or not: see [[stewards/obligations]]. Once you archive the project or drop it from the projects you steward, you have no CRA obligations left for it.

The practical step the guidance asks for is to say so clearly: mark the project as archived, inactive or end-of-life where its users will see it. The companies that integrate the project remain responsible for their own products and must do due diligence on the components they use ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]), so they need to know that nobody stewards it any more. A project doesn't need a steward ([[stewards/do-all-projects-have-stewards]]), and nothing in the CRA requires you to keep an inactive project alive or to find it new maintainers. Dropping a project doesn't make you its manufacturer either: the guidance says that only happens if you start monetising it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 84]]).

You don't risk anything for having stewarded a project that went inactive. If a market surveillance authority finds that you fell short of your own obligations while you were its steward, it can require corrective action, but stewards cannot be fined: see [[stewards/penalties]]. For a project that is still active but doesn't follow your policy, see [[stewards/project-ignores-policy]].
