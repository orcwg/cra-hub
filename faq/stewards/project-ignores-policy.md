# What happens if a project under a steward does not follow the steward's security policy?

If a project you steward ignores your cybersecurity policy, doesn't respond or refuses to fix vulnerabilities, you are not in breach of the CRA because of it, and you cannot be fined in any case. Your obligations as a steward are your own: to put in place and document a policy that _fosters_ secure development and effective vulnerability handling by the project's developers, to cooperate with market surveillance authorities at their request and, depending on how you support the project, to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents ([[CRA Article 24]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]). None of them requires the maintainers to follow your policy, and the CRA gives you no power over them: the maintainers of a project that isn't sold have no obligations under the CRA at all, towards you or towards anyone who integrates the project ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). The policy must _"take into account the specific nature of the open-source software steward and the legal and organisational arrangements to which it is subject"_ ([[CRA Article 24(1)]]), which includes a steward that cannot replace a project's maintainers.

The duty to cooperate with authorities is yours, so answer them yourself. If a market surveillance authority asks for clarifications about a vulnerability and the maintainers don't reply, it is you who must cooperate _"with a view to mitigating the cybersecurity risks"_ and provide your policy documentation on a reasoned request ([[CRA Article 24(2)]]). Do what is within your reach: share what you know, pass the request on to the maintainers, and tell the authority what the project's status is. Likewise, if you become aware of an actively exploited vulnerability, share it with the maintainers in line with your policy and consider reporting it voluntarily ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 80]], [[CRA Article 15]]); where you are involved in the project's development, you must report it, whether or not the maintainers act ([[CRA Article 24(3)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 82]]): see [[stewards/notification-obligations]].

What you do about the project is for your policy to say. Write down what happens when a project doesn't follow it: a process for revisiting the policy with the project, a way to flag the project as unmaintained or at risk, and, as a last resort, ending your stewardship of it. A steward that stops supporting a project is no longer its steward and should say so clearly ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 83]]): see [[stewards/inactive]]. A policy the maintainers helped write and are willing to follow does more for security than one imposed on them; the CRA asks you to foster good practice, not to police it ([[CRA Article 24(1)]]).

If a market surveillance authority finds that your policy or your cooperation falls short, it can require corrective action, but stewards cannot be fined: see [[stewards/penalties]].
