# Can a natural person's living expenses count as 'costs' or is that profit?

Yes. Reasonable living expenses count as costs, not profit. An individual (a natural person) who publishes open source software and charges for technical support to cover their costs, including reasonable living expenses, is not considered to be monetising that software on that basis alone ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]]).

So individual maintainers can accept payment for support to sustain themselves without automatically becoming manufacturers. If the support is optional and the software stays freely available, selling it does not make you a manufacturer ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]]). If what you sell is a supported version, with technical assistance bundled in, what matters is whether the price only recovers your actual costs, including a fair remuneration, or goes beyond them ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]]). The source code has to stay openly available either way: software whose source is only shared with paying customers is not open source under the CRA, whatever its licence, and is simply a product you sell ([[CRA Article 3(48)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 46]]).

Similarly, donations that help cover a natural person's reasonable living expenses are costs, not profit. Accepting donations without the intention of making a profit is not a commercial activity ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]).

For more on what counts as costs under the CRA, see [[cra-itself/actual-costs]]. For what constitutes profit, see [[cra-itself/profit]].
