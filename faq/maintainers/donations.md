# Does accepting donations make me a manufacturer?

No. Accepting donations does not make you a manufacturer, even when they add up to more than your costs.

The CRA says that _"accepting donations without the intention of making a profit should not be considered to be a commercial activity"_ ([[CRA Recital 15]]). The Commission's guidance adds that putting a donation link on your project is not a sign of an intention to make a profit, _"even where the amount collected via donations exceeds the mere costs"_ of developing and providing the software, and that a project supported only through donations is _"unlikely to be considered to be placed on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 20]]). Costs include reasonable compensation for the people a company or foundation employs on the project, and an individual's reasonable living expenses. Donations naturally fluctuate, so a degree of flexibility applies when assessing them.

The exception is when donating is really the price of the software. That is the case when access to the software, to essential features or to updates depends in practice on making a donation, or when donors get contractual benefits or exclusive advantages beyond community perks ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 62]]). Releases and security updates reserved for donors, or binaries and guaranteed fixes only for donors, are the examples the guidance gives of donations that count as a price ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 21]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 22]]). In that case you are selling the software, and you are its manufacturer.

For more on what counts as actual costs, see [[cra-itself/actual-costs]]. For how living expenses factor in, see [[maintainers/living-expenses]]. For the other ways a project can be monetised, see [[maintainers/monetization]] and [[cra-itself/monetisation]].
