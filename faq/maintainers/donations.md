# Does accepting donations make me a manufacturer?

No, receiving donations does not make you a manufacturer, as long as those donations cover your actual costs rather than generate profit.

The CRA says that _"accepting donations without the intention of making a profit should not be considered to be a commercial activity"_, while _"accepting donations exceeding the costs associated with the design, development and provision"_ of the software can be a sign of one ([[CRA Recital 15]]). Costs are broader than hosting and hardware: they include reasonable compensation for the people a company or foundation employs on the project, and an individual's reasonable living expenses ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]). When assessing whether donations exceed your costs, look at all the income the project brings in, not at one stream.

Putting a donation link on your project is not, by itself, an intention to make a profit, and the Commission's guidance says a project supported only through donations is _"unlikely to be considered to be placed on the market"_. Donations naturally fluctuate, so a degree of flexibility applies: what counts is the overall picture over time, not a month where they exceeded expenses ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 20]]).

Where donations clearly do make you a manufacturer is when donating is really the price of the software: access to it, to essential features or to updates depends in practice on making a donation, or donors get contractual benefits or exclusive advantages beyond community perks ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 62]]). Releases and security updates reserved for donors, or binaries and guaranteed fixes only for donors, are the guidance's examples ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 21]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 22]]). That is selling the software.

For more on what counts as actual costs, see [[cra-itself/actual-costs]]. For how living expenses factor in, see [[maintainers/living-expenses]]. For the other ways a project can be monetised, see [[maintainers/monetization]] and [[cra-itself/monetisation]].
