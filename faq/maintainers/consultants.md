# Do common consulting arrangements make me a manufacturer?

No, common consulting arrangements do not make you a manufacturer. Helping a client install, configure or integrate software is providing a service, not placing a product on the market. The CRA applies to products, not to services, so providing a service does not make you a manufacturer ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 59]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 19]], see [[manufacturers/service-providers]]).

This also holds for consulting around an open source project you publish yourself. Selling implementation, integration, training or other professional services for your own open source software does not, by itself, make you its manufacturer, as long as the software can be downloaded, installed and updated freely and your services are optional ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 55]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 18]]). What would make you a manufacturer is selling the software itself, for example a version whose updates or technical assistance only paying customers get: see [[maintainers/technical-support]] and [[maintainers/monetization]].

It is worth noting one exception: a service that substantially modifies open source software and provides it to customers without providing your modifications publicly under an open source licence makes you a manufacturer of the derived work ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 59]], [[CRA Article 22(1)]]).
