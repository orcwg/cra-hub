# Should I give my project to a steward to avoid obligations under the CRA?

No. If you maintain an open source project and don't monetise it, the CRA does not apply to you (see [[maintainers/no-monetization]]). There is nothing for a steward to take over.

A steward's obligations are its own, not a project's obligations handed to someone else. An _open-source software steward_ is a company or foundation that systematically supports specific open source software meant for commercial use ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]). The obligations of [[CRA Article 24]] fall on it because of that support, and cover its own conduct: a cybersecurity policy, cooperation with authorities, vulnerability reporting (see [[stewards/obligations]]).

Nothing in the CRA requires you to do any of those, nor to hand over your project for that. A foundation that hosts your repositories, runs your releases or handles your security reports can be the project's steward while you keep control of it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]; see [[stewards/relationship]]).

If you monetise your project, you are its manufacturer, and a steward cannot take that over: a steward is by definition _"other than a manufacturer"_ ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]).

The reasons to work with a steward are what it brings: infrastructure, security handling, funding, and documentation that helps the companies building on your project with their due diligence ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 79]]; see [[due-diligence/support]]). For setting up your own company instead, see [[maintainers/incorporate]].
