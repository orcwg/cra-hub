# Do I have obligations as a maintainer if my open source project is used in commercial or critical products?

No. As the maintainer of an open source project you don't monetise, you have no obligations under the CRA, and that does not change when companies build your project into their products, however critical those products are and whether you know of those uses or not. The Commission's guidance says that _"maintainers of FOSS components that are not placed on the market do not bear obligations in relation to other entities that may integrate such components into their products with digital elements"_: whether the CRA applies to your project depends only on whether you place it on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). The CRA does not look at who uses your project, how many, or how critical their products are: see [[maintainers/popularity]].

The obligations run the other way. A company that builds your project into a product it sells is that product's manufacturer. It has to comply with the CRA for the product, exercise due diligence on your project as a component and, when it finds a vulnerability in your project, report it to you and share its fix ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]], [[official/faq_4-4-4]]). An email from a car or medical device manufacturer is that company doing its due diligence. You are under no obligation to answer it, act on its reports or merge its fixes. What helps them, should you want to: [[projects/practices-to-adopt]] and [[due-diligence/support]]. How to tell them where you stand: [[maintainers/transparency]].

Your status changes only if you monetise your project. That makes you the manufacturer of the version you sell, and the category of that version depends on its own core functionality, not on the products it ends up in: see [[maintainers/become-manufacturer]] and [[important-and-critical-products/what-qualifies]]. If the project is published by a company or foundation rather than by you as an individual, that organisation may be its _open-source software steward_, and learning that companies build on the project bears on whether it is: see [[stewards/business-context]].
