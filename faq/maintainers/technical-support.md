# Does providing technical support for a fee put you in scope of the CRA?

Providing technical support for a fee puts you in scope of the CRA only when a version of the software, or its updates, is available solely with that support. Whether that is the case depends on your relationship to the project.

**If you publish the project:** offering paid support does not, by itself, put you in scope. Support, consulting, training and similar professional services around software that anyone can download, install and update for free are services, not a sale of the software ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 55]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 18]]). What puts you in scope is when a version of the software, or its updates and maintenance, is only available with paid support: that version is a product you sell, and you are its manufacturer ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 17]]). And if the source code itself is only available to paying customers, the software is not open source under the CRA, whatever its licence; it is simply a product you sell ([[CRA Article 3(48)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 46]]). The CRA counts paid support as monetisation only where the price _"does not serve only the recuperation of actual costs"_ ([[CRA Recital 15]]); for an individual (a natural person), those costs include reasonable living expenses and a fair remuneration, so a supported version whose price only covers them does not make you a manufacturer ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]]). If you are a company or foundation that meets the definition of an open-source software steward, you have the steward's obligations, rather than the manufacturer's, for the free version ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]).

**If you are a not-for-profit organisation** set up so that all earnings after costs go to your not-for-profit objectives, the software you publish is not considered sold even if you charge for support. If you meet the definition of a steward, you have the steward's obligations ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 66]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 77]]).

**If you do not publish the project:** you are a service provider, and services are not products under the CRA. Helping a customer install open source software on their server, without distributing the software yourself, does not put you in scope ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 59]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 19]]). The exception is a service that substantially modifies the software, which can make you its manufacturer ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 59]], [[CRA Article 22]]).

See also: [[maintainers/living-expenses]] and [[manufacturers/service-providers]].
