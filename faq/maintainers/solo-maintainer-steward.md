# Can a solo maintainer be considered to be an _open-source software steward_?

No. As defined in [[CRA Article 3(14)]], an open-source software steward must be a legal person (e.g. a company, a foundation, an association) in contrast with a natural person (i.e. a human being). The obligations of open-source software stewards described in [[CRA Article 24]] therefore do not apply to solo maintainers acting in their personal capacity. The Commission's guidance says the same: open source software that is not placed on the market is outside the CRA _"unless the entity publishing it is a legal person that meets the definition of 'open-source software steward'"_, and _"if the entity is a natural person, the version provided for free … is not within the scope of the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 43]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]).

This holds whatever you do with the project. The Commission's guidance settles donations: an individual developer who publishes a library, adds a link to collect donations and keeps the project maintained _"has no obligations under the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 34]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]), and regular releases do not make the software commercial either ([[CRA Recital 18]]). Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance says anything about using your own software in your day job, or about a disclaimer that the project comes with no promise of support. In the hub's reading neither matters: an individual cannot be a steward whatever the software is used for, and the software stays outside the CRA as long as you do not monetise it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]; on tools made for your own use, [[official/faq_1-5]]). Whether software is 'intended for commercial activities' only matters for companies and foundations: see [[stewards/business-context]].

However, should a solo maintainer set up a single-member company that is a legal entity in its own right, distinct from the natural legal persona of the solo maintainer, that entity could qualify as an open-source software steward if it meets the criteria for doing so. The same goes for a freelancer or sole trader whose legal form counts as a legal person in their country (see [[legislation/legal-person]]): even then, the Commission's guidance says, the software has to be intended for commercial activities, such as integration into services or products they sell, and supported on a sustained basis ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]], see [[stewards/business-context]]).

It is also worth noting that natural persons who monetise their project become subject to the CRA as manufacturers. In that case, they face the same obligations as any other manufacturer under [[CRA Article 13]], not the lighter-touch obligations that apply to open-source software stewards under [[CRA Article 24]]. For more on what qualifies as monetisation, see [[maintainers/monetization]].
