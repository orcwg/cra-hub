# What makes a maintainer of an open source project a manufacturer?

A maintainer of an open source project becomes its manufacturer when they monetise it, and only for the version they monetise. The Commission's guidance says that where the person supplying the software _"charges a price for the software itself […] that person is placing a product with digital elements on the market"_ and _"is therefore a manufacturer under the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]]).

Charging a price is one way to monetise. Making access to a version of the software, or to its updates, conditional on paying counts too, as does publishing it as a platform, such as a marketplace or store, through which you monetise other products or services, requiring the processing of users' personal data as a condition for use for reasons other than security, compatibility or interoperability, or taking donations that are in practice the price of access ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 54]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 62]]). Selling optional support around software anyone can download freely does not ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]]). What does and does not count is covered in [[maintainers/monetization]] and [[maintainers/technical-support]].

A paid version and a free "community" version of the same code are different products. You are the manufacturer of the paid version only; the free version _"is not monetised and therefore is not considered to be placed on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 52]]). If you are an individual, the free version is outside the CRA altogether; if you are a company or foundation, you are its _open-source software steward_, with the lighter obligations of [[CRA Article 24]] ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]; see [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]]).

A maintainer who does not monetise has no obligations under the CRA: the definition of a manufacturer covers software supplied _"free of charge"_ ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]), but only when it is supplied commercially, and open source software _"not monetised by [its] manufacturers should not be considered to be a commercial activity"_ ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]).
