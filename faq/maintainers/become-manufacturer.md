# What makes a maintainer of an open source project a manufacturer?

A maintainer becomes the manufacturer of an open source project when they monetise it, and only for the version they monetise: the paid version, not a free version of the same code. The Commission's guidance settles this: _"Whether the CRA applies to a given FOSS component depends solely on whether the natural or legal person that publishes it places it on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]), and placing open source software on the market means monetising it, because _"the provision of products with digital elements qualifying as free and open-source software that are not monetised by their manufacturers should not be considered to be a commercial activity"_ ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 42]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]]).

The guidance checks two things in turn ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Section 3.1]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Section 3.2]]). First, the project has to be under your responsibility: you publish it and have _"primary control over its development, releases, and distribution decisions"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). That is what being a maintainer means under the CRA; who holds the copyright or has commit access does not decide it, see [[maintainers/who-is-responsible]]. Second, you have to supply the software in the course of a commercial activity, which is what the CRA calls placing it on the market, and which for open source means monetising it ([[CRA Article 3(22)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 50]]). The CRA's definition of a manufacturer covers software supplied _"whether for payment, monetisation or free of charge"_ ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]), but the guidance reads it together with placing on the market: a manufacturer is the person who supplies the software under their name _"in the course of a commercial activity"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 47]]). So a maintainer who does not monetise the project has no obligations under the CRA ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]]).

Monetising means getting paid for the software itself: charging for it or for its binaries ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]]), reserving a version, its updates or technical assistance for paying users ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]]), using it as a platform to sell other services, requiring users' personal data for other reasons than security or interoperability ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 54]]), or donations that are in practice the price of access ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 62]]). Being paid to work on the project, taking donations that cover your costs, or selling optional support around software that anyone can download and update for free does not count ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]]). The details are in [[maintainers/monetization]], [[maintainers/paid-dev]], [[maintainers/donations]] and [[maintainers/technical-support]].

Once you monetise, you are the manufacturer of the paid version, with the obligations of [[CRA Article 13]], but only of that version. A free "community" version with (almost) the same code is a different product under the CRA: it is not monetised, so it is not placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 52]]). If you are an individual, that free version is outside the CRA altogether; if you are a company or foundation, you are its open-source software steward, with the lighter obligations of [[CRA Article 24]] ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 76]], see [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]]). A not-for-profit organisation set up so that all earnings after costs go to its not-for-profit objectives is a steward even for software it monetises ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 66]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 24]]).

Monetising also makes you the manufacturer only from the date you start. The guidance says so for a steward that starts monetising its project: it is the manufacturer _"from the date on which it places it on the market (but not in respect of earlier versions for which it acted as a steward)"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 84]]). Versions you published before you monetised stay as they were.
