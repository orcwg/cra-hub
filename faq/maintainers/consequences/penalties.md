# What penalties could I face as the maintainer of an open source project?

As a maintainer, you face no penalties under the CRA, because the CRA does not apply to you. It applies to manufacturers, and an individual who publishes open source software without monetising it is not one ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]; see [[maintainers/no-monetization]]). There is nothing to fine, whatever your project's security practices: no SBOM, security policy or formal process is required of you.

Penalties only come into play if you monetise your project, which makes you its manufacturer, with a manufacturer's obligations ([[maintainers/become-manufacturer]]). The CRA's enforcement then applies to you as to any manufacturer: an order to put things right first, and fines with the ceilings set in [[CRA Article 64]]. See [[manufacturers/mistakes]] and the [[manufacturers]] FAQs.

Those ceilings are maximums written with companies in mind, and the CRA scales fines down for the small. The size of the offender, microenterprises, small and medium-sized enterprises and start-ups in particular, is one of the factors a fine has to take into account ([[CRA Article 64(5)(c)]]). For an individual, so are their economic situation and the general level of income in their country ([[CRA Recital 121]]). Microenterprises and small enterprises cannot be fined at all for missing the 24-hour early warning deadline, and no other financial penalty can replace that fine ([[CRA Article 64(10)(a)]], [[CRA Recital 120]]). For what that looks like in practice, see [[manufacturers/enforcement-sme]] and [[maintainers/enforcement]].
