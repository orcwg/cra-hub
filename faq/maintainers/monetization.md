# Am I subject to the CRA if I earn a living from the open source project I maintain?

It depends on how you earn it, not on how much.

- **Donations**: no. Donations do not make you a manufacturer, even when they bring in more than your costs ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]). See [[maintainers/donations]] for the one exception, donations that are really the price of the software.
- **Paid services around freely available software**, such as consulting, training, integration or optional support: no. The software is not a product you sell as long as anyone can download, install and update it without paying ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 18]]). See [[maintainers/consultants]] and [[maintainers/technical-support]].
- **Sponsorship, grants or paid development** of features that you then release to everyone: no. How the development was financed does not count ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 65]]).
- **Selling the software itself**: yes. If you charge for the software, for a paid edition, or for access to updates, maintenance or technical assistance that only paying users get, you are selling a product and you are its manufacturer, with the obligations of [[CRA Article 13]] ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]]). For an individual there is one exception: support bundled with access that only recovers your actual costs, including a reasonable salary or [living expenses][], does not count as monetisation ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]]).

Even when you sell a version, your manufacturer obligations apply only to that version. If you offer both a paid "enterprise" version and a free "community" version, you are the manufacturer of the enterprise version only ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 52]]). The community version stays outside the CRA if you are an individual; if you are a company or foundation, you are its open-source software steward instead ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]], see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]]).

See [[cra-itself/actual-costs]] for what counts as actual costs, and [[cra-itself/monetisation]] for the other forms monetisation can take, such as monetising other services through the software or requiring users' personal data.
