# Am I subject to the CRA if I earn a living from the open source project I maintain?

Not as long as what you earn from the project only covers your actual costs, which include a reasonable salary or [living expenses][] ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]). See [[cra-itself/actual-costs]] for what counts.

What the money is for matters more than how much it is. Money paid for the software itself makes you its manufacturer: a price for the software or its binaries, a version whose updates or support only paying users get, or donations that are in practice the price of access ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 62]]). What counts as monetising is covered in [[maintainers/become-manufacturer]], which also says that it applies only to the version you charge for.

Money that is not for the software does not count, whatever the amount. Sponsorship, grants or paid development of features you release to everyone are how the work is financed, which the CRA does not take into account ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]]). Paid consulting, training, integration or optional support around software anyone can download and update for free are services, not a sale ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]]). See [[maintainers/paid-dev]], [[maintainers/grants]], [[maintainers/consultants]] and [[maintainers/technical-support]]. Donations that cover your costs do not count either: see [[maintainers/donations]].

If you sell a paid product built on your project, such as an "enterprise" edition or a plugin, you are the manufacturer of that product. The free version stays outside the CRA, as it does for any individual who supplies it for free ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 52]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]).
