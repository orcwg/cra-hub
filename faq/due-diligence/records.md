# What records of my due diligence on components do I have to keep?

Records that show, for each component, what your product needed from it and how you verified that it delivers it, kept up to date for the support period. The CRA prescribes no template or format. It requires the technical documentation to demonstrate how the product meets the essential requirements, with the risk assessment in it ([[CRA Article 31]], [[CRA Annex VII]], [[CRA Article 13(4)]]), and it requires you to _"systematically document, in a manner that is proportionate to the nature and the cybersecurity risks, relevant cybersecurity aspects"_ of the product, including vulnerabilities you become aware of and information third parties give you ([[CRA Article 13(7)]]).

Due diligence is its own obligation, and the Commission's guidance says it _"supports and underpins"_ your ability to demonstrate compliance for the product as a whole ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 171]]). The guidance also says what the evidence can be: the requirements you identified for the component in the light of your risk assessment, documentation obtained from the component's maker, such as technical specifications, security documentation or conformity and assurance documentation, and your own tests where appropriate ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 170]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 171]]). The Commission's FAQ lists the usual checks: the component's security update history, its known vulnerabilities, its SBOM where it has one, its support period and whether its intended purpose fits yours ([[official/faq_4-4-2]]). The product's SBOM is itself one of the records ([[CRA Annex I, Part II]]).

The technical documentation is kept for ten years or the support period, whichever is longer, and given to a market surveillance authority on reasoned request, in a language it understands ([[CRA Article 13(13)]], [[official/faq_4-1-8]]). It is not public, except for open source products in an important category whose manufacturer self-assesses ([[official/faq_6-6]]). The harmonised standards, as they are published, will set out the level of detail expected ([[standards/harmonised-standards]]). See [[due-diligence/what-is-due-diligence]] and [[due-diligence/foss]].
