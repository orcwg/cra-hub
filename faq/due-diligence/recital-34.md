# Are the actions listed in Recital 34 a complete list of what due diligence on components requires?

No, the actions listed in Recital 34 are examples, not a complete list. The Commission's FAQ presents them as _"examples of one or more actions that manufacturers may undertake"_ and adds _"Additional examples"_ the recital doesn't name: software composition analysis, sandboxing or isolating highly critical components, reviewing the component's SBOM where it has one, checking its support period, checking that its intended purpose fits the use you make of it, and assessing the security posture of whoever makes it ([[official/faq_4-4-2]]). What the CRA requires of you is an outcome: the components you integrate, including open source components, must _"not compromise the cybersecurity of the product"_ ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Recital 34]]).

How much checking that takes depends on the component. The Commission's FAQ says the level of due diligence _"depends on the nature and level of cybersecurity risk of a given component"_, that your product's risk assessment informs it, and that a component carrying more risk calls for more extensive actions than one carrying fewer ([[official/faq_4-4-2]]). The four actions the recital names are: checking whether the component's manufacturer has demonstrated conformity with the CRA, for instance through the CE marking; checking that it receives regular security updates, such as by looking at its security update history; checking that it is free from vulnerabilities registered in the European vulnerability database or other public vulnerability databases; and carrying out additional security tests ([[CRA Recital 34]]).

The Commission's guidance describes the method for choosing between them. Due diligence means _"determining what the product with digital elements requires from its components in order to meet its cybersecurity objectives, and verifying, in a risk-based manner, that those components are in line with the product's needs"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 170]]). If your product relies on a component for cryptography, updates or secure communications, you identify those needs and check that the component meets them, using the component's documentation (technical specifications, security documentation, conformity or assurance documents) and, where appropriate, your own tests ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 171]]). Which of Recital 34's actions, or others, are the right way to do that follows from what the component does in your product, not from the list.

Due diligence does not mean bringing the component into compliance with Annex I before you use it: see [[due-diligence/essential-requirements]]. For how this applies to open source components specifically, see [[due-diligence/foss]].
