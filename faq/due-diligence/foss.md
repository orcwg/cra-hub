# What level of due diligence is reasonable for open source components?

The level of due diligence reasonable for an open source component is the same as for any other third-party component: enough checking, in proportion to the component's risk and to what your product relies on it for, to make sure the component doesn't compromise the security of your product ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[official/faq_4-4-4]]). The CRA sets no fixed list of checks and no stricter rule for open source.

Article 13(5) names open source only to make clear that the obligation covers components that are not sold or otherwise placed on the market: due diligence applies _"including when integrating components of free and open-source software that have not been made available on the market in the course of a commercial activity"_ ([[CRA Article 13(5)]]). You may integrate open source components that are outside the scope of the CRA, as well as components published by an open-source software steward ([[official/faq_4-4-4]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]). The guidance's scenarios all end the same way: whether the project is an individual with a donation link, a foundation, or a company's unmonetised project, the company that integrates it _"needs to exercise due diligence in accordance with Article 13(5)"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 28]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 34]]).

What due diligence requires is to work out, from your product's risk assessment, what your product needs from the component, and to verify in a risk-based way that the component provides it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 170]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 171]]). Recital 34's actions are examples of how to do that, and the Commission's FAQ lists more: see [[due-diligence/recital-34]]. For an open source component, the checks that fit are typically whether the project is maintained and ships security updates (its security update history), whether vulnerabilities in the European vulnerability database or other public databases affect the version you integrate, what the project's security documentation says about the functions you rely on, its SBOM where it has one, how long the project commits to supporting the version you use, and your own tests where the risk warrants them ([[CRA Recital 34]], [[official/faq_4-4-2]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 171]]). A component your product depends on for cryptography or updates needs more of this than a formatting library.

Three things are different about open source in practice:

- **There is usually no CE marking to rely on.** An open source component that isn't placed on the market has no CE marking and its developers have no obligations under the CRA, so the first of Recital 34's actions doesn't apply and you use the others ([[official/faq_4-4-1]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 34]]). This does not mean you have to bring the component into compliance with Annex I yourself: see [[due-diligence/essential-requirements]].
- **Projects can make this easier.** A project with a steward has a documented cybersecurity policy covering secure development and vulnerability handling ([[CRA Article 24]]), which gives you something to check. Any project can publish documentation that helps you verify what you rely on: see [[due-diligence/support]]. The CRA also lets the Commission set up voluntary security attestation programmes for open source components, precisely to _"facilitate the due diligence obligation"_ ([[CRA Article 25]], [[CRA Recital 21]]); none exists yet, and integrating only attested components is not required: see [[attestations/what-is-a-security-attestation]].
- **Vulnerability handling stays with you.** Your vulnerability handling obligations cover your product in its entirety, including every integrated component ([[CRA Recital 34]], [[official/faq_4-3-6]]). If you find a vulnerability in an open source component, you report it to the project, fix it in your product, and share your fix with the project ([[CRA Article 13(6)]]). Where the project doesn't fix it, you address it by other means: disabling the function, switching components, or patching it yourself ([[official/faq_4-3-6]]).
