# What does "Monetizing without making a profit" mean?

Monetising your open source project without making a profit means taking in money for it, such as donations or payment for technical support, that only covers your actual costs. Doing so is not a commercial activity, so it does not make you the project's manufacturer ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]).

If you are an individual maintainer, your actual costs include your reasonable living expenses, so being paid fairly for the hours you work on the project does not count as making a profit ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]]). For a company or foundation, they include reasonable pay for the contributors it hires ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]). The costs that count are listed in [[cra-itself/actual-costs]].

Donations get extra flexibility. A link to a donation platform is not a sign of intending to make a profit, even when donations exceed your costs, and a project supported only through donations is _"unlikely to be considered to be placed on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]). Donations are covered in [[maintainers/donations]].

Some forms of monetisation make you a manufacturer whatever you earn from them: charging for the software itself, using it to monetise other services, or requiring users' personal data for reasons other than security, compatibility or interoperability ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 54]]). These are described in [[cra-itself/monetisation]] and [[cra-itself/profit]].
