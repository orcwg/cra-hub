# What is a _harmonised standard_ and why does it matter?

A _harmonised standard_ is a European standard that the Commission has asked the European standardisation organisations (CEN, CENELEC and ETSI) to develop for an EU law, and whose reference the Commission has published in the Official Journal of the European Union. It matters because of the _presumption of conformity_: a product that follows a harmonised standard _"shall be presumed to be in conformity with the essential cybersecurity requirements set out in Annex I covered by those standards or parts thereof"_ ([[CRA Article 27(1)]]). For most manufacturers, harmonised standards will be the practical way to show that a product complies.

The presumption goes only as far as the standard. The Commission's FAQ says harmonised standards _"do not replace legally binding essential requirements"_ and that manufacturers _"remain fully responsible for assessing all the risks of their product"_; where a standard covers only part of the requirements, the rest has to be met through other technical specifications or the manufacturer's own solutions ([[official/faq_4-1-7]]). Following a standard is voluntary: a manufacturer can meet the requirements another way, and document how.

The harmonised standards for the CRA are still being written. The Commission's standardisation request asks for 15 horizontal standards, clustered in three deliverables due between August 2026 and October 2027, and 26 vertical standards for the important and critical product categories, due by October 2026; the Commission then assesses each one before publishing its reference ([[official/faq_6-10]], [[CRA Article 27(6)]]). The ORC WG keeps a [list of the standards requested][standards].
