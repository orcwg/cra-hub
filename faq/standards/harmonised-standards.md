# What is a _harmonised standard_ and why does it matter?

A _harmonised standard_ is a European standard that one of the [European Standardisation Organisations (ESOs)][ESO] adopts at the request of the European Commission to support an EU law. For the CRA, the Commission has requested a set of them from CEN, CENELEC and ETSI ([[official/faq_6-10]]); the ORC WG maintains a [summary of that request, with every standard requested and its deadline][standards].

Harmonised standards matter because of the _presumption of conformity_. Once the Commission has published a standard's reference in the [Official Journal of the European Union][OJEU], products that conform with it _"shall be presumed to be in conformity with the essential cybersecurity requirements set out in Annex I covered by those standards or parts thereof"_ ([[CRA Article 27(1)]]). Certain (but not all) harmonised standards are referenced in this way, sometimes with restrictions, in which case the presumption is partial. Where a standard covers only part of the requirements, the manufacturer has to meet the rest through other technical specifications or its own solutions ([[official/faq_4-1-7]]).

The presumption is a convenience, not a substitute for the law. The Commission's FAQ says harmonised standards _"do not replace legally binding essential requirements"_ and that manufacturers _"remain fully responsible for assessing all the risks of their product"_ ([[official/faq_4-1-7]]). Following a standard is voluntary: a manufacturer can meet the requirements another way and document how.
