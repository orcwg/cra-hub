# When is open source software "under the responsibility" of a person or company?

Open source software is under the responsibility of those who publish it and control it. The Commission's guidance says it is _"under the responsibility"_ of the _"natural or legal persons who publish it and exercise primary control over its development, releases, and distribution decisions (often referred to as 'maintainers')"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). The phrase comes from [[CRA Recital 18]], which keeps the CRA away from anyone who contributes code to open source software that is not under their responsibility.

The test is control over the project, not ownership of the code or access to the repository. The guidance says that _"persons who contribute source code but do not control releases, roadmaps, or governance decisions are considered 'contributors'"_, and that _"the mere existence of technical permissions, such as commit access, is not sufficient"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). Someone whose pull request the maintainers review, merge and release is a contributor, whatever they contributed ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 13]]); the guidance does not mention copyright at all. Where several people hold that control together, the software is under their responsibility together; a company's employees holding some of the merge rights or steering group seats does not by itself put the project under the company's control.

Being responsible for the software only settles who the CRA could apply to. Whether it does depends on the next question, whether that person supplies the software in the course of a commercial activity ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 50]]): an individual or company that publishes and controls a project without monetising it has no obligations, and a company may instead be its _open-source software steward_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]). See [[maintainers/contributors]], [[maintainers/become-manufacturer]] and [[stewards/whose-codebase]].
