# Does the CRA regulate open source projects, or the people and organisations behind them?

Neither as such: the CRA regulates products _made available on the market_ ([[CRA Article 2(1)]]) and the _natural or legal persons_ who supply them, the manufacturers, importers and distributors, plus the companies and foundations that are _open-source software stewards_. The Commission's guidance says that whether it applies to open source software _"depends solely on whether the natural or legal person that publishes it places it on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]).

How the software is developed does not come into it. The CRA says that _"the mere circumstances under which the product with digital elements has been developed, or how the development has been financed, should therefore not be taken into account"_, so as to keep _"a clear distinction between the development and supply phases"_ ([[CRA Recital 18]]). Sharing code on a public repository is not in itself placing it on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 23]]). What matters is who is responsible for the product ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]; see [[cra-itself/under-responsibility]]) and whether they monetise it:

- A project an individual publishes without monetising it: nobody is regulated ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]]).
- A project a foundation publishes and sustains for commercial use: the foundation is its steward, with the light obligations of [[CRA Article 24]] ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 28]]).
- A project a company publishes and maintains under its own name without monetising it: the company is its steward, not its manufacturer, even if it uses the project in its own products ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]]).
- A project someone monetises: that person is its manufacturer ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]]).

Companies that only contribute to or fund a project are not regulated for it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]]). See [[manufacturers/do-all-projects-have-manufacturers]] and [[stewards/do-all-projects-have-stewards]].
