# What does 'actual costs' mean under the CRA?

Under the CRA, 'actual costs' are the legitimate expenses of designing, developing, maintaining and providing software. You can recover them through donations or through fees for technical support without that counting as a commercial activity ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]).

For **individual maintainers**, actual costs include:
- Expenses related to the design, development and maintenance of the software
- **Reasonable living expenses** — an individual who covers their costs and earns a fair remuneration through support services or donations is not considered to be monetising the software on that basis alone ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]])

For **companies and foundations** (legal persons), actual costs include:
- Costs associated with the design, development and provision of the software
- Reasonable compensation for the contributors and developers the organisation employs ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]])

Recovering actual costs is not a commercial activity. Earning more than your costs is not automatically one either: what matters is how the money comes in. Donations collected through a link on your project are not a sign that you intend to make a profit, even when they add up to more than your costs. They become a price when access to the software, to essential features or to updates depends on donating, or when donors get exclusive benefits beyond community perks ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 62]]). Fees for technical support count as monetisation when access to the software or to its maintenance is conditional on paying them and the price goes beyond recovering actual costs ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]]).

Donations naturally fluctuate over time, so a degree of flexibility applies when assessing them: an occasional month where income exceeds expenses does not make a project commercial ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]).
