# What does 'actual costs' mean under the CRA?

Under the CRA, 'actual costs' are the legitimate expenses of designing, developing, maintaining and providing open source software. You can recover them through various means (including donations and technical support) without that counting as a commercial activity ([[CRA Recital 15]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]).

For **individual maintainers**, actual costs include:
- Expenses related to the design, development and maintenance of open source software
- **Reasonable living expenses** — an individual who covers their costs and earns a fair remuneration through support services or donations is not considered to be monetising the software on that basis alone ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 58]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]])

For **companies and foundations** (legal persons), actual costs include:
- Costs associated with the design, development and provision of the open source software
- Reasonable compensation for the contributors and developers the organisation employs ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]])

Recovering actual costs is not a commercial activity. Taking in significantly more than your costs, with the intention of making a profit, can be one: the CRA names _"accepting donations exceeding the costs associated with the design, development and provision"_ of the software, and charging for technical support beyond the recovery of actual costs, as signs of a commercial activity ([[CRA Recital 15]]). The clearest case is when paying is in practice the price of the software: access to it, to essential features or to updates depends on donating or on buying support, or donors get exclusive benefits beyond community perks ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 56]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 62]]).

Donations naturally fluctuate over time, so a degree of flexibility applies when assessing them: whether you intend to make a profit is judged on the overall picture over time, not on a month where income exceeded expenses. A project supported only through donations is unlikely to be considered placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 61]]).
