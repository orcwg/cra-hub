# What are market surveillance authorities and how do they operate?

Market surveillance authorities are the national bodies that check whether products on the EU market comply with EU product law, and that enforce it when they don't. For the CRA, each Member State designates one or more, new or existing, with the staff, cybersecurity skills and resources to do the job ([[CRA Article 52(2)]], [[CRA Article 52(8)]]). They operate under the EU's general market surveillance rules, Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, which the CRA applies to products with digital elements ([[CRA Article 52(1)]]).

They act on products already on the market. An authority can ask a manufacturer, on a reasoned request, for the documentation it needs to check a product's design, development, production and vulnerability handling ([[CRA Article 53]]); where it has reason to think a product presents a significant cybersecurity risk, it evaluates it, with the CSIRT where appropriate, and requires the manufacturer to correct it ([[CRA Article 54]]). The Commission's Q&A sums up its powers: it can _"require operators to bring the non-compliance to an end and eliminate the risk, to prohibit or restrict the making available of a product on the market, or to order that the product is withdrawn or recalled"_, and fine companies within the CRA's ceilings ([[cra-basics/what-will-happen-to-non-compliant-products]], [[CRA Article 64]]). It can act even on a compliant product that still presents a significant risk ([[CRA Article 57]]).

For open source, the same authorities supervise _open-source software stewards_. Where a steward falls short of [[CRA Article 24]], the authority requires corrective action, and stewards cannot be fined ([[CRA Article 52(3)]], [[CRA Article 64(10)(b)]]). Authorities also give guidance to companies, tell consumers where to complain, and coordinate across the EU in the ADCO ([[CRA Article 52(10)]], [[CRA Article 52(11)]], [[CRA Article 52(15)]]).
