# Who is in charge of enforcing the CRA?

The CRA is enforced by the _market surveillance authorities_ of the EU Member States, not by the Commission or ENISA. The Commission's Q&A says _"Member States will appoint market surveillance authorities, which would be responsible for the enforcement of the Cyber Resilience Act obligations"_ ([[cra-basics/what-will-happen-to-non-compliant-products]]). Each Member State designates one or more, new or existing ([[CRA Article 52(2)]]), and they are the same authorities that check manufacturers' products and the obligations of _open-source software stewards_ ([[CRA Article 52(3)]]).

They work within the EU's general market surveillance rules, Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, which the CRA makes applicable to products with digital elements ([[CRA Article 52(1)]]), and they coordinate across the EU in a dedicated administrative cooperation group, the ADCO, so that the regulation is applied the same way everywhere ([[CRA Article 52(15)]]). The authorities can ask ENISA and the national CSIRTs for technical advice, and ENISA and the CSIRTs pass them what they learn from reported vulnerabilities and incidents, but the decision to act is the authority's ([[CRA Article 52(4)]], [[CRA Article 52(5)]]).

Penalties are national too: Member States lay down the rules and apply the fines, within the ceilings the CRA sets ([[CRA Article 64]]). What an authority can do when it finds a problem, and what that looks like in practice: see [[cra-itself/market-surveillance-authorities]], and for stewards [[stewards/enforcement]].
