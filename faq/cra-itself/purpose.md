# What is the purpose of the CRA?

The purpose of the CRA is a more secure and resilient market for the EU's citizens and businesses: products with digital elements that reach the EU market with fewer vulnerabilities, and manufacturers that keep them secure for as long as they are in use. The Commission's first Q&A on the CRA puts it in three points: the CRA will ensure that connected products and software placed on the EU market _"are more secure"_, that _"manufacturers remain responsible for the cybersecurity of a product throughout its lifecycle"_, and that _"consumers are properly informed about the cybersecurity of the products they buy and use"_ ([[cra-basics/what-is-the-new-eu-cra]]).

The regulation itself opens with the problem: _"the number and variety of connected devices will rise exponentially in the coming years"_, cyberattacks have _"a critical impact not only on the Union's economy, but also on democracy as well as consumer safety and health"_, and two things add costs for users and society, _"a low level of cybersecurity of products with digital elements"_ and _"the insufficient and inconsistent provision of security updates"_ ([[CRA Recital 1]]). Its stated aim is products _"placed on the market with fewer vulnerabilities"_, manufacturers that _"take security seriously throughout a product's lifecycle"_, and users able to _"take cybersecurity into account when selecting and using"_ them ([[CRA Recital 2]]). The Commission's Q&A says who gains: fewer incidents, one set of rules across the EU, and better informed consumers ([[cra-basics/who-will-benefit-from-the-cra]]).

Open source is not the CRA's target; it comes into the picture because most products are built on it. How the CRA treats it: see [[cra-itself/open-source]]. What the CRA is: see [[cra-itself/cra]].
