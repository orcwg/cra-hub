# Do programming language interpreters fall into the operating system category?

No. Interpreters, compilers and language runtimes are not listed among the important or critical product categories. They are in the default category, like most products in scope: see [[important-and-critical-products/what-qualifies]].

The Commission can add a category to those lists on its own, by delegated act ([[CRA Article 7(3)]]), but has not done so for interpreters.

Interpreters are not operating systems, which the implementing regulation describes as products _"that provide an abstract interface of the underlying hardware and control the execution of software"_ ([[CRA-CATEGORIES Annex I]]). Containing a component or having the functionalities of an important or critical product category doesn't place a product in that category if its core functionality is different ([[official/faq_3-4]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 141]], [[CRA-CATEGORIES Recital 5]]).

The category only decides the conformity assessment procedure, not how much security work the product needs. The risk assessment and the essential requirements apply to every product in scope and scale with its own risk: the Commission's FAQ says that _"irrespective of whether the product with digital elements is considered to be an important or critical product"_, manufacturers carry out a comprehensive cybersecurity risk assessment ([[official/faq_3-3]], [[CRA Article 13(2)]]). An interpreter that much other software depends on still needs a thorough risk assessment, even though it can assess its own conformity under module A, like every product in the default category ([[official/faq_6-1]]): see [[manufacturers/self-assessment]]. A product in the default category can end up with more stringent security requirements than an important one.
