# What is the difference between self-assessment and third-party conformity assessment?

In self-assessment, the manufacturer checks its own product against the CRA's requirements and declares conformity on its own responsibility; in third-party assessment, an independent _notified body_ has to examine the product, or the manufacturer's quality system, before the manufacturer can declare conformity. The CRA calls neither "certification": both are _conformity assessment procedures_ under [[CRA Article 32]], set out in [[CRA Annex VIII]].

The Commission's FAQ describes self-assessment, module A, as the procedure in which _"the manufacturer verifies that the product with digital elements complies with the essential requirements of the CRA and declares compliance on its sole responsibility"_, with _"no notified bodies"_ involved ([[official/faq_6-1]]). In modules B+C and H _"a notified body examines the design and development of the product"_, or assesses the manufacturer's full quality control system ([[official/faq_6-2]], [[official/faq_6-3]]). Notified bodies are private or public entities whose competence a Member State has checked, independent of manufacturers and authorities ([[official/faq_6-9]]).

Which procedure applies depends on the product's category ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 137]], [[official/faq_6-1]]):

- products in the default category, which is most of them, can always self-assess;
- important products of class I can self-assess if they apply a harmonised standard covering their core functionality, and otherwise need a notified body ([[CRA Article 32(2)]]);
- important products of class II and critical products need a notified body ([[CRA Article 32(3)]], [[CRA Article 32(4)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 148]]);
- open source products in any important category can self-assess, provided their technical documentation is public when the product is placed on the market ([[CRA Article 32(5)]]).

This only concerns products placed on the market: a project that is not monetised has no conformity assessment to do at all. A self-assessment that is wrong is a non-compliant product: market surveillance authorities can require corrective action, restrict or withdraw the product and fine the manufacturer ([[cra-itself/market-surveillance-authorities]]). What happens when a manufacturer gets it wrong: see [[manufacturers/mistakes]].
