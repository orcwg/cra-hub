# If a company is a major contributor to an open source project but doesn't monetise it, is it the manufacturer?

No. Being a major contributor to an open source project does not make a company its manufacturer, however much of the code it writes.

The Commission's guidance says that a manufacturer which integrates an open source component _"does not become responsible for such components' individual compliance with the CRA, even where the manufacturers contribute source code to their maintenance"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]]). The CRA does not apply to anyone who contributes code to open source software _"not under their responsibility"_ ([[CRA Recital 18]]), and software is under the responsibility of those who publish it and control its development, releases and distribution; commit access alone is not enough ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). In the guidance's own example, a manufacturer that funds and contributes features to a foundation's project _"does not become the manufacturer of that FOSS component"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]).

What makes a company a manufacturer is marketing the software under its own name or trademark in the course of a commercial activity ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 47]]), for instance by charging for it or monetising it through other services. See [[manufacturers/contributors]] and [[cra-itself/monetisation]].

If the company does publish and control the project without monetising it, it may be the project's open-source software steward instead: a legal person that supports the project on a sustained basis and ensures its viability, where the project is intended for commercial use ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]]). See [[stewards/what-is-a-steward]].
