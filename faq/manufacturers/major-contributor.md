# If a company is a major contributor to an open source project but doesn't monetise it, is it the manufacturer?

No. Being a major contributor to an open source project does not make a company its manufacturer, however much of the code it writes.

The Commission's guidance says that a manufacturer which integrates an open source component _"does not become responsible for such components' individual compliance with the CRA, even where the manufacturers contribute source code to their maintenance"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]]), and its example of a manufacturer that funds and contributes features to a foundation's project ends the same way: it _"does not become the manufacturer of that FOSS component"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]). What decides is who is responsible for the project, those who _"publish it and exercise primary control over its development, releases, and distribution decisions"_; commit access alone is not enough ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]]). Supporting or contributing to a project in general: see [[manufacturers/contributors]].

What makes a company a manufacturer is marketing the software under its own name or trademark in the course of a commercial activity, for instance by monetising it ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 47]]): see [[manufacturers/what-is-a-manufacturer]] and [[cra-itself/monetisation]].

If the company does publish and control the project without monetising it, it may be the project's _open-source software steward_ instead: a company or foundation that supports the project on a sustained basis and ensures its viability, where the project is intended for commercial use ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]]). See [[stewards/what-is-a-steward]].
