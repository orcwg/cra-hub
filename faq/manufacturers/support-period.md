# What is the required support period for a product under the CRA?

At least five years, and longer when the product is expected to be in use for longer. The support period is the time during which the manufacturer has to handle the product's vulnerabilities ([[CRA Article 13(8)]]).

The Commission's FAQ says the period _"needs to be set to at least five years, but that is not sufficient where products with digital elements are reasonably expected to be in use for longer than five years"_ ([[official/faq_4-5-2]]). The Commission's guidance adds that _"a support period of five years is therefore not to be considered as the default"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 126]]). The manufacturer works out the expected use time from reasonable user expectations, the nature and intended purpose of the product, EU law setting the lifetime of such products, and the support periods of similar products and of integrated components ([[official/faq_4-5-1]]). The reasoning goes into the technical documentation.

A shorter period is only allowed when the product is expected to be in use for less than five years, as with software that stops working when a subscription ends. The FAQ notes that open source software monetised only through paid support subscriptions is such a case, with a support period _"equal to the duration of the active subscription"_ ([[official/faq_4-5-2]], [[CRA Recital 60]]).

Software released in successive versions gets a support period for each substantially modified version placed on the market. The manufacturer may fix vulnerabilities only in the latest version if users of earlier versions can upgrade to it free of charge and without additional costs ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 128]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 129]]).

The support period is a manufacturer's obligation: an _open-source software steward_ does not set one ([[CRA Article 24]]). For who is a manufacturer, see [[manufacturers/what-is-a-manufacturer]]; for what placing on the market means, see [[cra-itself/placing-on-the-market]].
