# What is a _manufacturer_?

The term _Manufacturer_ is defined in [[CRA Article 3(13)]] of the CRA:

> ‘manufacturer’ means a natural or legal person who develops or manufactures products with digital elements or has products with digital elements designed, developed or manufactured, and markets them under its name or trademark, whether for payment, monetisation or free of charge;

This is the manufacturer of EU product law, the same definition every New Legislative Framework act uses. The Blue Guide states it as _"any natural or legal person who manufactures a product or has a product designed or manufactured, and places it on the market under his own name or trademark"_ (section 3.1). Where the CRA says the manufacturer _markets them under its name or trademark_, the Blue Guide says _places it on the market under his own name or trademark_: same act, different words. The CRA defines _placing on the market_ and _making available on the market_ as those acts are defined throughout EU product law: supply on the EU market in the course of a commercial activity ([[CRA Article 3(21)]], [[CRA Article 3(22)]]; see [[cra-itself/placing-on-the-market]]). The Commission's guidance says the same: the manufacturer is the person that supplies a product under its name or trademark _"in the course of a commercial activity (thereby placing it on the market)"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 47]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 71]]).

So developing software and putting your name on it does not make you its manufacturer. Supplying it in the course of a commercial activity does, which for open source software means monetising it: see [[cra-itself/monetisation]] and [[stewards/developing]]. For the framework the definition comes from, see [[legislation/nlf]] and [[legislation/blue-guide]].
