# Can a manufacturer place CRA obligations on an open source project by supporting it, financially or otherwise?

No, a manufacturer cannot place CRA obligations on an open source project or its maintainers by supporting it, whether with money, a contract for development work or its own developers' time. The Commission's guidance says so in terms: _"Maintainers of FOSS components that are not placed on the market do not bear obligations in relation to other entities that may integrate such components into their products with digital elements. Whether the CRA applies to a given FOSS component depends solely on whether the natural or legal person that publishes it places it on the market"_, in other words sells or otherwise monetises it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). How the project is financed is not taken into account ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 64]]). Its scenario of companies that donate to the developer of a project they integrate ends the same way: _"Developer A has no obligations under the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]]).

That holds even when the support comes with a contract. The guidance says that _"the fact that a commercial entity may have paid for that project should not contribute to determining whether the software is placed on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 65]]): a manufacturer that pays a maintainer to add a feature, fix bugs or maintain a component is buying development work, not the software, and when the maintainer releases the result to everyone the project is still not placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 23]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]). The contract can say what work is delivered; it cannot make the maintainer a manufacturer. See [[maintainers/paid-dev]] and [[manufacturers/service-providers]].

What the manufacturer owes, it owes for its own product. The Commission's FAQ says manufacturers _"are allowed to integrate open-source components that are not in scope of the CRA"_, with a level of due diligence that depends on the component's risk ([[official/faq_4-4-4]]), and that a manufacturer _"does not need to bring such components into compliance with the essential requirements set out in Annex I, part I, before integrating them"_: what it has to make sure of is that its own product is secure and meets the CRA ([[official/faq_4-4-3]], [[CRA Article 13(5)]]). The same goes for fixing vulnerabilities. The project's developers are not subject to the CRA's vulnerability handling obligations, and where they are _"not supporting the manufacturer in addressing and remediating vulnerabilities, the integrating manufacturer is expected to address the vulnerability via other means"_, such as patching the component itself; it then has to share the fix with the project ([[official/faq_4-3-6]], [[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]). A manufacturer that wants more assurance about a project can ask for it, fund it or, once the Commission sets up security attestation programmes, pay for an attestation itself ([[CRA Recital 21]], [[official/faq_4-4-4]]); it cannot require it. See [[due-diligence/support]] for how a project can help, if it chooses to.

The situation is different when the "support" is really a purchase of the software: a paid edition, or updates and maintenance available only to paying customers. The Commission's guidance treats that version as placed on the market, _"irrespective of whether functionally equivalent software is also available free of charge under a free and open-source licence"_: the company selling it is its manufacturer, with the manufacturer's obligations towards its customers ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 17]]). That comes from the seller monetising the software, not from the buyer's money. See [[maintainers/technical-support]].

One nuance for stewards: regular contributions or financing from manufacturers can show that a project is _"intended for commercial activities"_ ([[CRA Recital 19]]), one of the conditions for the company or foundation that publishes it to be its _open-source software steward_, with the steward's lighter obligations ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA Article 24]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 28]]). Those obligations fall on the publishing organisation, not on the manufacturer supporting it, and never on an individual maintainer, who cannot be a steward: see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]].
