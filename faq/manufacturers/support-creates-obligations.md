# Can a manufacturer place obligations on a project or its maintainers by supporting it, financially or otherwise?

No. A manufacturer cannot put CRA obligations on an open source project or its maintainers by supporting it, whether with money, code or anything else.

The Commission's guidance says that _"maintainers of FOSS components that are not placed on the market do not bear obligations in relation to other entities that may integrate such components into their products with digital elements"_, and that whether the CRA applies to a component _"depends solely on whether the natural or legal person that publishes it places it on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). How a project is financed does not count, so grants, sponsorships, bug bounties or paid development work don't change its status ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]]).

The obligations stay with the manufacturer. For its own product it has to comply with the CRA, exercise due diligence on the open source components it integrates, even those not placed on the market, and report the vulnerabilities it finds in them upstream and share its fixes ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]). Funding a feature that is then released to everyone changes nothing ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 23]]): see [[manufacturers/funding-foss-dev]].

The project only takes on obligations if its maintainers place it on the market themselves, for instance by monetising it. Buying a commercial version with guaranteed fixes or support from a company makes that company the manufacturer of that version, because it sells it, not because the buyer supports the project ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]]). See also [[manufacturers/contributors]].
