# Can a manufacturer place CRA obligations on an open source project by supporting it, financially or otherwise?

No, a manufacturer cannot place CRA obligations on an open source project or its maintainers by supporting it, whether with money, a contract for development work or its own developers' time. The obligations for the manufacturer's product stay with the manufacturer.

Whether the CRA applies to a project _"depends solely on whether the natural or legal person that publishes it places it on the market"_, in other words sells or otherwise monetises it ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). How the project is financed is not taken into account ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 64]]). The Commission's guidance states it plainly: _"maintainers of FOSS components that are not placed on the market do not bear obligations in relation to other entities that may integrate such components into their products with digital elements"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]).

That holds even when the support comes with a contract. A manufacturer that pays a maintainer to add a feature, fix bugs or maintain a component is buying development work, not the software; when the maintainer releases the result to everyone, the project is still not placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 65]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 23]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]). The contract can say what work is delivered; it cannot make the maintainer a manufacturer. See [[maintainers/paid-dev]] and [[manufacturers/service-providers]].

What the manufacturer owes, it owes for its own product. It has to make that product comply, exercise due diligence on the open source components it integrates, _"including when integrating components of free and open-source software that have not been made available on the market"_, report the vulnerabilities it finds in them to the project and share its fixes ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]). The guidance's scenarios of companies that fund or contribute to a project they use all end with the companies exercising due diligence and the project owing them nothing ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 23]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]). The Commission's FAQ on integrating open source components that are not subject to the CRA confirms this is allowed, with a level of due diligence that depends on the component's risk ([[official/faq_4-4-4]]). A manufacturer that wants more assurance about a project can ask for it, fund it or, once the Commission sets up security attestation programmes, pay for an attestation itself ([[CRA Recital 21]]); it cannot require it. See [[due-diligence/support]] for how a project can help, if it chooses to.

The situation is different when the "support" is really a purchase of the software: a paid edition, or updates and maintenance available only to paying customers. The company selling that version is its manufacturer, with the manufacturer's obligations towards its customers ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 17]]). That comes from the seller monetising the software, not from the buyer's money. See [[maintainers/technical-support]].

One nuance for stewards: regular contributions or financing from manufacturers can show that a project is _"intended for commercial activities"_ ([[CRA Recital 19]]), one of the conditions for the company or foundation that publishes it to be its _open-source software steward_, with the steward's lighter obligations ([[CRA Article 3(14)]], [[CRA Article 24]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 78]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 28]]). Those obligations fall on the publishing organisation, not on the manufacturer supporting it, and never on an individual maintainer, who cannot be a steward: see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]].
