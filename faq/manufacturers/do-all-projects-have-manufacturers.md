# Do all open source projects have a _manufacturer_?

No. Most open source projects have no manufacturer. The Commission's guidance says a project has one only when the person or organisation publishing it sells it: _"Whether the CRA applies to a given FOSS component depends solely on whether the natural or legal person that publishes it places it on the market"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]), and a project that is not placed on the market _"falls outside the scope of the CRA"_, unless the organisation publishing it meets the definition of an open-source software steward ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 43]]). The Commission's FAQ takes the same for granted when it speaks of open source components _"that are not in scope of the CRA (i.e. because they are not made available on the market in the course of a commercial activity)"_ ([[official/faq_4-4-4]]).

_Placing on the market_ means supplying the software in the EU in the course of a commercial activity, and the guidance confirms that publishing open source software without monetising it is not one ([[CRA Article 3(22)]], [[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 42]]). What counts as monetising a project: see [[maintainers/monetization]] and [[cra-itself/monetisation]]. Nothing in the CRA requires a project to identify or appoint a manufacturer: the status follows from what the publisher does.

Most open source projects are not monetised by the people who publish them, so most have no manufacturer. Many have no steward either: a steward has to be a company, foundation or other legal person that systematically supports the project on a sustained basis, and most projects have no such organisation behind them (see [[stewards/do-all-projects-have-stewards]]). In the guidance's examples, an individual who publishes and maintains a project without charging for it, donations or not, _"has no obligations under the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 34]]).

This stays true however widely the project is used in commercial products. The guidance is explicit: the companies that build it into something they sell are the manufacturers of their own products, and their doing so _"has no impact on the status of that FOSS component under the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]; see [[manufacturers/multiple-manufacturers]]). Nor does financial support or contributed code from such companies give the project a manufacturer ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]]; see [[manufacturers/major-contributor]]).

For users, this means there is nobody to hold to manufacturer obligations for an open source project that nobody sells. The CRA's protection applies where a company sells a product built on it. The Commission's FAQ says such a company may integrate the project but _"must ensure that such components do not compromise the cybersecurity of their products with digital elements"_ ([[official/faq_4-4-1]], [[official/faq_4-4-4]]), and has to handle vulnerabilities _"for their products in their entirety, including by handling vulnerabilities affecting their products that are contained in integrated components"_ for the whole of its support period ([[official/faq_4-3-6]], [[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Article 13(8)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]).
