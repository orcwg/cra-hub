# If two companies build and ship the same open source project as part of their products, are both manufacturers?

Neither company is the manufacturer of the open source project. Each is the manufacturer of its own product, including the copy of the project built into it.

A manufacturer is whoever markets a product with digital elements _"under its name or trademark, whether for payment, monetisation or free of charge"_ ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]). When two companies independently build, brand and sell products containing the same open source code, each places its own product on the market, and the Commission's guidance says that manufacturers which integrate open source components _"are required to comply with the CRA for their own products with digital elements"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]).

So each company is responsible, for its own product, for:

- meeting the essential cybersecurity requirements and carrying out the conformity assessment ([[official/faq_4-4-3]]);
- exercising due diligence on the open source component it integrates ([[CRA Article 13(5)]]);
- handling vulnerabilities throughout its support period, including reporting an actively exploited vulnerability in the shared component: the Commission's FAQ says every manufacturer integrating the component has to notify it ([[official/faq_5-4]]);
- reporting vulnerabilities it finds in the component upstream and sharing the fix ([[CRA Article 13(6)]]).

The project's own status does not change: it has a manufacturer only if whoever publishes it monetises it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]), and often it has none. See [[manufacturers/do-all-projects-have-manufacturers]] and [[manufacturers/what-is-a-manufacturer]].
