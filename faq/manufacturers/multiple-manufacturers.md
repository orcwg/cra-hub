# If two companies build and ship the same open source project as part of their products, are both manufacturers?

Yes. Each company is the manufacturer of its own product, including the copy of the open source project built into it. Neither is the manufacturer of the project itself, and there is no conflict to resolve: manufacturer status attaches to each product someone sells, not to the codebase it is built from.

A manufacturer is whoever supplies a product under its own name or trademark in the course of a commercial activity ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA Article 3(22)]]). A company that takes open source code and builds it into something it sells is placing a new product of its own on the market, and is _"the manufacturer of that new product with digital elements for the purposes of the CRA and is required to comply with the Regulation in its entirety in respect of the product with digital elements as a whole"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 120]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 51]]). Two companies doing this are two manufacturers of two products. Each has the full obligations of [[CRA Article 13]] for its own product, open source components included: it has to handle vulnerabilities in the product as a whole for its support period ([[CRA Article 13(8)]]), and report an actively exploited vulnerability in it even when the vulnerability comes from a component the other company also ships ([[official/faq_5-4]]). What one company does for its product neither helps nor binds the other.

The same applies to a company that packages the project and ships it, patched or not, as part of a product it sells, such as a Linux distribution. That company is the manufacturer of its product, not a distributor of the project: a distributor passes on a product that someone else has already placed on the market, _"without affecting its properties"_ ([[CRA Article 3(17)]]), and a distributor that sells under its own name or trademark, or substantially modifies the product, is treated as a manufacturer anyway ([[CRA Article 21]]).

Shipping the project does not make either company responsible for it. Manufacturers that build open source components into their products _"do not become responsible for such components' individual compliance with the CRA, even where the manufacturers contribute source code to their maintenance"_, and the maintainers of a project that is not sold _"do not bear obligations in relation to other entities that may integrate such components into their products with digital elements"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). What each company owes the project is limited to due diligence on the component when it integrates it ([[CRA Article 13(5)]]; see [[due-diligence/what-is-due-diligence]]) and, when it finds a vulnerability in it, reporting it to the project and sharing any fix it develops ([[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 222]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 226]]). It has no obligation to support the project for people who are not its customers: its security updates are for its own product and its own users.

The project itself is unaffected. If nobody sells it, it has no manufacturer, however many products are built on it (see [[manufacturers/do-all-projects-have-manufacturers]]). If its publisher sells it, that publisher is the manufacturer of the version it sells, alongside the companies that are manufacturers of theirs. And if the project stops being maintained, each company still has to fix vulnerabilities in its own product by other means, for example by patching the component itself or replacing it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 225]], [[official/faq_4-3-6]]).
