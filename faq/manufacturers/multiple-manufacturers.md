# If two companies build and ship the same open source project as part of their products, are both manufacturers?

Yes. Each company is the manufacturer of its own product, including the copy of the open source project built into it. Neither is the manufacturer of the project itself. The Commission's guidance says a company that assembles components into a product it sells _"is placing a new product with digital elements of its own on the market"_ and _"is the manufacturer of that new product with digital elements for the purposes of the CRA and is required to comply with the Regulation in its entirety in respect of the product with digital elements as a whole"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 120]]). Two companies doing this are two manufacturers of two products, and there is no conflict to resolve: manufacturer status attaches to each product someone sells, not to the codebase it is built from ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]).

The Commission's FAQ answers the reporting side the same way. Asked whether all manufacturers integrating a component with an actively exploited vulnerability have to report it, it says each does for its own product: _"Where the product with digital elements contains an actively exploited vulnerability originating from an integrated component, the manufacturer of the product with digital elements is required to notify that vulnerability"_, unless the vulnerability cannot be exploited in that product; the project's own maintainers have to report it only _"if that component has been placed on the market"_ ([[official/faq_5-4]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 218]]). Each company also has to handle vulnerabilities _"for their products in their entirety, including by handling vulnerabilities affecting their products that are contained in integrated components"_, for the whole of its support period ([[official/faq_4-3-6]], [[CRA Article 13(8)]]). What one company does for its product neither helps nor binds the other.

The same applies to a company that packages the project and ships it, patched or not, as part of a product it sells, such as a Linux distribution. Neither the Commission's FAQ nor its guidance addresses distributions by name, but the guidance's rule on integration covers them: whoever _"assembles components (including, where appropriate, by modifying them) into a new product with digital elements that they themselves place on the market"_ is that product's manufacturer ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 120]]). A distributor in the CRA's sense only passes on a product that someone else has already placed on the market, _"without affecting its properties"_ ([[CRA Article 3(17)]]), and a distributor that sells under its own name or trademark, or substantially modifies the product, is treated as a manufacturer anyway ([[CRA Article 21]]).

Shipping the project does not make either company responsible for it. The guidance says manufacturers that build open source components into their products _"do not become responsible for such components' individual compliance with the CRA, even where the manufacturers contribute source code to their maintenance"_, and the maintainers of a project that is not sold _"do not bear obligations in relation to other entities that may integrate such components into their products with digital elements"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 87]]). What each company owes the project is limited to due diligence on the component when it integrates it ([[official/faq_4-4-4]], [[CRA Article 13(5)]]; see [[due-diligence/what-is-due-diligence]]) and, when it finds a vulnerability in it, reporting it to the project and sharing any fix it develops ([[official/faq_4-3-6]], [[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]). It has no obligation to support the project for people who are not its customers: its security updates are for its own product and its own users.

The project itself is unaffected. If nobody sells it, it has no manufacturer, however many products are built on it (see [[manufacturers/do-all-projects-have-manufacturers]]). If its publisher sells it, that publisher is the manufacturer of the version it sells, alongside the companies that are manufacturers of theirs ([[official/faq_5-4]]). And if the project stops being maintained, the Commission's FAQ says each company still has to fix vulnerabilities in its own product _"via other means, for example by disabling compromised functions; switching out the affected component; developing by itself a patch"_ ([[official/faq_4-3-6]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 225]]).
