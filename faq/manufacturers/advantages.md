# Are there circumstances where being a manufacturer may be advantageous?

Yes: only a manufacturer can put the CE marking on its product, and the CE marking is going to be read as a sign of security. The CRA makes it _"the only marking which guarantees that products with digital elements comply with the requirements set out in this Regulation"_ ([[CRA Recital 89]]); the Commission's FAQ describes it as the manufacturer's self-declaration of compliance, _"addressed to consumers and market surveillance authorities"_ ([[official/faq_6-7]]), and its Q&A expects the CRA to _"increase trust consumers and business customers have in companies and products, and so increase the demand"_ for them ([[cra-basics/who-will-benefit-from-the-cra]]).

That recognition may turn into a requirement. Companies subject to NIS 2 or DORA have to manage the security of what they buy and of their suppliers ([[NIS2 Article 21]], [[DORA Article 28]]), and public and private procurement may come to ask for the CE marking as the simplest evidence that software is secure. Some open source projects that are only stewards under the CRA are, for that reason, considering becoming manufacturers of their software in order to carry it.

That means placing the software on the market, typically by monetising it, and taking on a manufacturer's full obligations for the version concerned ([[CRA Article 13]]; see [[maintainers/become-manufacturer]] and [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]]). The CRA also offers an alternative for open source: voluntary _security attestations_, designed as the equivalent of the CE marking for open source software that is not placed on the market, to help manufacturers' due diligence and reassure users in the same way ([[CRA Recital 21]], [[CRA Article 25]], [[official/faq_4-4-4]]). No programme exists yet: see [[attestations/what-is-a-security-attestation]]. On the CE marking itself: [[cra-itself/ce-mark]].
