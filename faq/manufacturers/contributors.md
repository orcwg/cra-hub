# Does supporting or contributing to an open source project make my company its manufacturer?

No, supporting or contributing to an open source project does not make your company its manufacturer, even if your company is already the manufacturer of other products. The Commission's guidance says so in terms: manufacturers that integrate open source components _"do not become responsible for such components' individual compliance with the CRA, even where the manufacturers contribute source code to their maintenance"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]]), and _"a manufacturer that has contributed to the development of certain features does not become the manufacturer of that FOSS component"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]]).

You are the manufacturer of the software you publish and sell under your own name or trademark, and of nothing else ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]). The guidance looks at each piece of software on its own: being the manufacturer of one product says nothing about your role for another ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 72]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]).

**Money does not make you a manufacturer.** The guidance says that funding a project, whether through _"grants, bug bounties, sponsorships, service contracts or paid development work"_, does not in itself determine whether it is placed on the market, as the CRA itself says of how development is financed ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 65]]). See [[manufacturers/funding-foss-dev]].

**Code does not make you a manufacturer either.** The CRA does not apply to anyone, person or company, who contributes code to open source software that is not under their responsibility ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 85]]). The guidance puts a project under the responsibility of those who publish it and control its releases, roadmap and governance; contributing code, or even having commit access, is not enough ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 13]]). Its scenarios of companies that contribute developers' time to a project they use all end the same way: no obligations for that project ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 30]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 31]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 33]]). See [[maintainers/contributors]].

What you do owe is for your own product. The Commission's FAQ says you may integrate open source components that are not subject to the CRA, with a level of due diligence that depends on the component's risk ([[official/faq_4-4-4]]); you also have to report the vulnerabilities you find to the project and share your fixes with it ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]). Your support does not shift any of that onto the project: see [[manufacturers/support-creates-obligations]].

The picture changes only if your company is the one publishing the project under its own name and controlling it. Then the project is under your responsibility: you are its manufacturer if you monetise it, and may be its steward if you don't ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 67]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 68]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]]). See [[manufacturers/major-contributor]] and [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]].
