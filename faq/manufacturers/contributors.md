# If a manufacturer supports or contributes to an open source project, does that make them a manufacturer for that project?

No. Supporting or contributing to an open source project does not make you a manufacturer for that project, even if you are the manufacturer of other products.

The Commission's guidance says that manufacturers who integrate open source components into their products _"do not become responsible for such components' individual compliance with the CRA, even where the manufacturers contribute source code to their maintenance"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]]). Nor does funding a project make it a commercial activity ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]]).

What makes you a manufacturer is placing software on the market under your own name or trademark, for instance by monetising it ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]; see [[manufacturers/what-is-a-manufacturer]]). A project is the responsibility of those who _"publish it and exercise primary control over its development, releases, and distribution decisions"_; contributing code without that control leaves you outside the CRA for that project ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 13]]).

See [[maintainers/contributors]] for contributors in general, [[manufacturers/major-contributor]] for a company that does most of a project's work, and [[manufacturers/support-creates-obligations]] for what your support means for the maintainers.
