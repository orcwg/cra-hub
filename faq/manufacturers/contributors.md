# Does supporting or contributing to an open source project make my company its manufacturer?

No, supporting or contributing to an open source project does not make your company its manufacturer, even if your company is already the manufacturer of other products. You are the manufacturer of the software you sell under your own name or trademark; funding someone else's project, or contributing code to it, is neither ([[CRA Article 3(13)]], [[CRA Article 3(22)]]).

The CRA looks at each piece of software on its own. Being the manufacturer of one product says nothing about your role for another: for each project, what counts is whether you are the one publishing it and selling it ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 72]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 74]]).

**Money does not make you a manufacturer.** How a project is financed is not taken into account when deciding whether it is sold: _"the mere fact that an open-source software product with digital elements receives financial support from manufacturers or that manufacturers contribute to the development of such a product should not in itself determine that the activity is of commercial nature"_ ([[CRA Recital 18]]). The Commission's guidance says the same of grants, sponsorships, bug bounties, service contracts and paid development work ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 63]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 65]]). See [[manufacturers/funding-foss-dev]].

**Code does not make you a manufacturer either.** The CRA does not apply to anyone, person or company, who contributes code to open source software that is not under their responsibility ([[CRA Recital 18]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 85]]). A project is under the responsibility of those who publish it and control its releases, roadmap and governance; contributing code, or even having commit access, is not enough ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 49]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 13]]). The guidance is explicit that manufacturers that use an open source component in their products _"do not become responsible for such components' individual compliance with the CRA, even where the manufacturers contribute source code to their maintenance"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 86]]). Its scenarios of companies that contribute developers' time to a project they use all end the same way: no obligations for that project ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 30]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 31]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 32]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 33]]). See [[maintainers/contributors]].

What you do owe is for your own product. If you integrate the project into something you sell, you have to exercise due diligence on it, report the vulnerabilities you find to the project and share your fixes with it ([[CRA Article 13(5)]], [[CRA Article 13(6)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 88]]). Your support does not shift any of that onto the project: see [[manufacturers/support-creates-obligations]].

The picture changes only if your company is the one publishing the project under its own name and controlling it. Then the project is under your responsibility: you are its manufacturer if you monetise it, and may be its steward if you don't ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 67]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 68]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 29]]). See [[manufacturers/major-contributor]] and [[manufacturers/both-manufacturer-and-steward]].
