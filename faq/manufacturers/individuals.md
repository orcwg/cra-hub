# Can individuals be manufacturers?

Yes. The CRA defines a manufacturer as _"a natural or legal person"_ who develops a product with digital elements and markets it under their name or trademark ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]), and the Commission's guidance says the same: the manufacturer is _"the natural or legal person that supplies a product with digital elements under its name or trademark, and that does so in the course of a commercial activity"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 47]]). Being a company is not a condition.

For an individual publishing open source software, what decides is monetisation. If you monetise your project, you are its manufacturer, with the same obligations as any company ([[CRA Article 13]]): see [[maintainers/become-manufacturer]]. If you don't, your software is outside the CRA altogether: the guidance says the free version an individual supplies _"is not within the scope of the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]), and an individual who publishes a project with a donation link _"has no obligations under the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 27]]).

The lighter steward regime is not open to individuals, since a steward must be a legal person ([[CRA Article 3(14)]]): see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]]. What counts as monetising: [[maintainers/monetization]].
