# Are free app store listings considered 'making available on the market' under the CRA?

A free app store listing is _making available on the market_ only if you supply the app in the course of a commercial activity, that is, if you monetise it in some way. The Commission's guidance says so of this very case: a mobile app that a user downloads from an app store is a product with digital elements, and _"where it is placed on the market in the course of a commercial activity, it may fall within the scope of the CRA"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 3]]). Listing an open source app that you do not monetise is not, however many people download it.

What counts is the commercial activity, not the price. The definition covers supply _"whether in return for payment or free of charge"_, and the Commission's guidance, following the Blue Guide, reads commercial activity as providing goods _"in a business-related context"_ ([[CRA Article 3(22)]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 40]]). For open source, the guidance says that software _"not monetised by their manufacturers should not be considered to be a commercial activity"_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 42]], [[CRA Recital 18]]). The test is applied to each app and to the person supplying it, not to the store ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 11]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 50]]), so it makes no difference that the store also carries paid or closed-source apps. A free, unmonetised open source app on an app store is in the same position as the same app offered from your own website: outside the CRA.

A free listing does count as making available on the market when the free download is how you monetise something else. The Commission's guidance gives three examples of apps that are _"available free of charge"_ and nevertheless placed on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 54]]):

- a marketplace app through which its publisher earns advertising, commission or subscription fees ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 14]]);
- a VPN app whose users can pay for extra servers or dedicated IP addresses, that is, in-app purchases or a paid service reached through the app ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 15]]);
- a fitness app whose use is conditional on processing personal data for targeted advertising or analytics ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 16]]).

Charging for the app itself, for its binaries, or for a version whose updates or support are only available to paying users is a sale ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 51]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 57]]). In all of these cases the app is placed on the market the first time it is offered in the store, every later download is a further making available ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 13]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 14]]), and you are its manufacturer, _"whether for payment, monetisation or free of charge"_ ([[CRA Article 3(13)]]). See [[cra-itself/monetisation]] for the forms monetisation can take, and [[maintainers/monetization]] for what does and does not count as earning from your project.

The store itself does not change the answer. In the guidance's example above, the app store is only how the app reaches the user; the question stays whether you supply it commercially ([[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 3]]). The guidance says the same of open source code shared on public repositories, which _"is generally not considered to be placing that code on the EU market"_, and in its example of an individual's free library on a public package repository, neither the developer nor the repository has obligations under the CRA ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 23]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE Example 34]], [[CRA Recital 20]]).

If you are an individual, an unmonetised app is outside the CRA altogether. If you are a company or foundation, it is not placed on the market, but you may be its _open-source software steward_, with lighter obligations, if the app is intended for commercial activities ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 43]], [[CRA-GUIDANCE point 53]]): see [[maintainers/solo-maintainer-steward]] and [[stewards/what-is-a-steward]].

Two related points. The part of [[CRA Recital 18]] about components _"intended for integration by other manufacturers"_ is about libraries and other building blocks, not end-user apps; an app you publish to users is covered by the general rule above, that unmonetised open source is not a commercial activity (see [[projects/binaries]] for components). And a free app that is not open source cannot rely on that rule: for it, the Commission's guidance says whether the supply happens in a business-related context _"can only be appreciated on a case-by-case basis, taking into account the regularity of the supplies, the characteristics of the product, the intentions of the supplier, etc."_ ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 40]], recalling section 2.2 of the Blue Guide): see [[cra-itself/freeware]].
