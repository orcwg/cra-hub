# What is the _New Legislative Framework_ (NLF)?

The New Legislative Framework (NLF) is the EU's standard model for product legislation, and the model the CRA follows. The Commission's FAQ describes it as three legal acts: _"Regulation (EC) 765/2008 setting out the requirements for accreditation and the market surveillance of products; Decision 768/2008 on a common framework for the marketing of products; and Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance and compliance of products"_ ([[official/faq_4-1-1]]).

Under this model, a law sets _essential requirements_ a product must meet and leaves the technical detail to harmonised standards. The manufacturer is responsible for assessing conformity, drawing up technical documentation and affixing the CE marking before placing the product on the market, and market surveillance authorities check products once they are on the market. The Blue Guide explains the whole system, and the Commission's guidance relies on it for the concepts the CRA shares with other product laws, such as placing on the market ([[CRA-GUIDANCE point 11]]).

The CRA is built from the NLF's parts: its conformity assessment procedures are the modules of Decision 768/2008 ([[CRA Recital 90]]), its CE marking follows Regulation (EC) 765/2008 ([[CRA Recital 89]]), and its market surveillance applies Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 ([[CRA Article 52]]). The Commission's Q&A puts it simply: the CRA is _"based on the New Legislative Framework for product legislation"_, so manufacturers undergo a conformity assessment, by self-assessment or through a third party depending on the product's risk ([[cra-basics/how-does-the-cra-address-these-problems]]).
